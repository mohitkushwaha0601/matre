Part I · Framework

The coast, its guardians and its command map

Snapshot and how to read

India's coast is a long, populated and economically busy edge: 13 coastal states and Union Territories, over 1,000 islands, a dozen major ports and many smaller ones, and the sea-lanes that carry the great majority of India's trade by volume. It is also the front line for threats that arrive from the sea, from the 1993 explosive landings to the 26/11 attack of 2008. This handbook follows the coast region by region.

The coastal landscape

The traditional figure of about 7,516 km for India's coastline (mainland plus islands) is the one most exam and official texts use. A later remeasurement at a finer scale gives a figure near 11,100 km, which shows how a coastline's length depends on method. Re-verify Gujarat has the longest mainland coastline. The country has about 1,300 islands, mostly in the Andaman and Nicobar group (Bay of Bengal) and Lakshadweep (Arabian Sea). Re-verify counts

  • West coast: a narrow plain below the Western Ghats, with natural harbours such as Mumbai and Kochi, the Konkan and Malabar stretches, backwaters in Kerala, and the Gulfs of Kutch and Khambhat in Gujarat.
  • East coast: a wider plain called the Coromandel in the south and the Northern Circars in Andhra, with large deltas (Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri, Ganga-Brahmaputra), lagoons such as Chilika and Pulicat, and fewer natural harbours.
  • Hazards: most Indian Ocean cyclones affecting India form in the Bay of Bengal and strike the east coast, with surge and flooding; the Arabian Sea has become more active in recent years. Tsunami risk, erosion and rising sea level affect both coasts. Interpretation of trends
  • Mangroves: the Sundarbans is the largest mangrove forest in the world, shared with Bangladesh, and acts as a natural buffer against storm surge.

Who guards the coast

No single agency guards the coast. The system is layered by distance from shore and by type of task, and after 26/11 the Navy was designated the authority responsible for overall maritime security, including coastal and offshore security. Re-verify exact wording of the 2009 arrangement

The detailed structure, ranks and ships of the Navy and Coast Guard are in handbooks 1 and 24. The important regional point is overlap: a suspicious boat seen by a fisher may be handled by the Marine Police at 3 nm, the Coast Guard at 20 nm and the Navy beyond.

Why layers exist

The 26/11 attackers crossed a coast that several agencies each thought another was watching. Post-2008 reforms tried to remove such gaps through joint operations centres, shared tracking and clearer responsibility rather than by adding one new agency.

Part II · Region by region

From the Kutch creeks to the Sundarbans, and the islands

Gujarat and the north-west coast

Gujarat has the longest mainland coastline of any state and the country's busiest cluster of ports. It faces Pakistan across the Kutch creeks and the northern Arabian Sea, with Karachi relatively close to the Kutch and Saurashtra coast, so coastal security here has a strong border-and-intelligence character. The Coast Guard's North West Region is headquartered at Gandhinagar.

  • Ports: Kandla (Deendayal Port, a major port), Mundra (large private port with the biggest cargo volumes among Indian ports), Pipavav, Dahej, Hazira, Okha, Porbandar and Dwarka. The Jamnagar refining complex is among the largest in the world, and the Alang yard near Bhavnagar is a major ship-breaking site. Re-verify rankings
  • Navy and Coast Guard: INS Valsura at Jamnagar (technical training), naval and Coast Guard presence at Okha and Porbandar, and Coast Guard stations and air enclaves along the coast. Re-verify unit locations
  • Sir Creek: a roughly 96 km tidal estuary in the Rann of Kutch between Gujarat and Pakistan's Sindh. India and Pakistan disagree on where the boundary runs along or through the creek, which affects maritime limits beyond its mouth. The issue has never been settled. Disputed
Risks

Narcotics and arms smuggling from the Makran coast, boat intrusions and fishermen detained across the line (both sides hold fishermen), chemical and oil terminals concentrated near the coast, and cyclones such as Biparjoy in 2023. The 26/11 attackers hijacked the Indian fishing boat Kuber off Porbandar before heading to Mumbai.

Maharashtra and Goa

Mumbai is the centre of gravity of India's west coast: the headquarters of the Western Naval Command, the Naval Dockyard, the Coast Guard's Western Region, the country's financial capital and its largest container port complex. It is also the city hit by the 1993 serial blasts, for which explosives were landed on the Raigad coast, and by the 26/11 sea-borne attack. Established

  • Shipbuilding: Mazagon Dock Shipbuilders builds the Project 15B destroyers, Project 17A frigates and Scorpène-class (Kalvari) submarines, while Goa Shipyard at Vasco builds patrol vessels and other warships for the Navy and Coast Guard. Re-verify delivery status
  • Ports: Mumbai Port, JNPT at Nhava Sheva (India's largest container port by traffic) and Mormugao in Goa. A new deep-water port at Vadhavan in Palghar district was approved in 2024. Re-verify
  • Goa: INS Hansa near Dabolim is a major naval air station and shares its airfield with the civil airport; Goa was liberated from Portuguese rule in December 1961 through Operation Vijay, in which the Navy took part.
  • Offshore: the Mumbai High oil and gas fields lie off the coast and need protection from the sea.
  • Maritime heritage: Shivaji's navy and Kanhoji Angre's fleet used the Konkan forts, including Sindhudurg; this heritage is cited in the Navy's current symbols.

Why Mumbai matters to the exam answer

It combines three layers in one place: a major naval command, a dense commercial port and a proven target. That combination is one reason the post-2008 reforms placed a joint operations centre here.

Karnataka and Kerala

Karnataka

Karwar hosts INS Kadamba, the base built under Project Seabird. It was designed to relieve congestion at Mumbai and to give the Western Fleet a deep, sheltered base with room for large ships such as aircraft carriers; later phases of expansion have been planned. Re-verify phase status Mangaluru (New Mangalore Port, a major port, and the MRPL refinery) and the Coast Guard's district presence complete the picture. The Kali, Sharavati and other west-flowing rivers meet the sea here, and the Kaiga nuclear plant is inland on the Kali.

Kerala

Kochi is the headquarters of the Southern Naval Command, with the Navy's flying school at INS Garuda and the shipyard that built INS Vikrant, India's first indigenous aircraft carrier, commissioned in September 2022. Vizhinjam, near Thiruvananthapuram, is a deep-water international transhipment port that began operations in 2024 and was formally commissioned in 2025. It aims to capture container transhipment that now goes through Colombo. Re-verify dates and capacity

  • Enrica Lexie (2012): two Italian marines on a tanker shot dead two Indian fishermen off Kerala. An international tribunal in 2020 held that Italy's marines had immunity, while India was entitled to compensation. Established The case is a standard example of coastal jurisdiction and the EEZ.
  • Vulnerabilities: coastal erosion, floods, a large fishing population and the dense shipping lane just offshore. Interpretation

Tamil Nadu and Puducherry

Tamil Nadu has a long coast on the Bay of Bengal and the Gulf of Mannar and meets the Arabian Sea and the Indian Ocean at Kanniyakumari, the southern tip of the mainland. It faces Sri Lanka across the narrow Palk Strait. Chennai is the headquarters of the Coast Guard's Eastern Region and a major port.

  • Ports and yards: Chennai, Kamarajar (Ennore) and V. O. Chidambaranar (Tuticorin) are major ports; L&T's Kattupalli yard near Chennai builds Coast Guard and other vessels. Re-verify
  • Naval air presence: INS Rajali at Arakkonam, a major naval air station, and INS Parundu near Uchipuli, with coastal Coast Guard air and surface stations at Mandapam and elsewhere. Re-verify
  • Fisher issue and Katchatheevu: Tamil Nadu's fishermen routinely cross the maritime boundary in the Palk Bay, where fishing grounds are small, and are arrested by the Sri Lankan Navy. India ceded the islet of Katchatheevu to Sri Lanka by the 1974 and 1976 agreements, which remains politically contested in Tamil Nadu. Bottom trawling by Indian boats is also a point of friction. Disputed
  • Adam's Bridge and Sethusamudram: the shoals between Rameswaram and Mannar are the site of the long-debated Sethusamudram ship-canal project, now stalled over environmental and cultural objections.
  • Nuclear sites: Kalpakkam and Kudankulam on the coast need maritime protection and are exposed to tsunami risk, a lesson of 2004.

Puducherry's Karaikal enclave is on the Tamil Nadu coast, and Mahe is on the Kerala coast, which is why the Union Territory has coasts on both seas.

Andhra Pradesh and Odisha

This stretch holds the east coast's naval heart and the country's most cyclone-exposed shoreline. Visakhapatnam is the headquarters of the Eastern Naval Command and home to the Eastern Fleet, the Naval Dockyard, INS Dega air station and the submarine arm, including the training school INS Satavahana. It is also where India's nuclear ballistic-missile submarines are built at the Ship Building Centre, and a dedicated naval base, Project Varsha, has been developed near Rambilli to the south. Re-verify

  • Submarines: INS Arihant (the first SSBN, commissioned 2016, publicly confirmed on its first deterrence patrol in 2018) and INS Arighaat (commissioned in 2024) are associated with Visakhapatnam. Detail on India's deterrent is not public. Re-verify
  • Ports: Visakhapatnam (major), Gangavaram, Kakinada and Krishnapatnam in Andhra; Paradip (major), Dhamra and Gopalpur in Odisha. Coast Guard stations at Visakhapatnam, Kakinada, Krishnapatnam, Paradip and Gopalpur form the local layer.
  • Missile testing: DRDO's range at Dr. APJ Abdul Kalam Island (formerly Wheeler Island) off Odisha and the Chandipur test range host many missile trials, which is why the sea and airspace there are regularly restricted.
  • Cyclones: the 1999 Odisha super cyclone killed about ten thousand people; Hudhud (2014) hit Visakhapatnam hard; Fani (2019) saw about 1.2 million people moved to shelters with low loss of life, widely cited as a success of early warning. Re-verify figures
  • Ecology: Gahirmatha has one of the world's largest olive ridley turtle nesting sites, and Bhitarkanika has mangroves, both sensitive to port and test-range activity.

West Bengal and the Sundarbans

West Bengal's coast is short and low, built from the Ganga-Brahmaputra delta. Kolkata (Syama Prasad Mookerjee Port) and Haldia lie upstream on the Hooghly, so the ports depend on continuous dredging and pilotage. Garden Reach Shipbuilders and Engineers at Kolkata builds frigates, corvettes and survey ships for the Navy, and the Coast Guard's North East Region is headquartered at Kolkata.

  • Sundarbans: the largest mangrove forest in the world, shared with Bangladesh, home to the Royal Bengal tiger and protected as a UNESCO site. It is a wetland border where the BSF Water Wing, the Coast Guard and state police operate on small creeks and rivers.
  • Boundary with Bangladesh: in 2014 an arbitral tribunal under UNCLOS settled the India-Bangladesh maritime boundary in the Bay of Bengal, awarding most of the disputed area to Bangladesh. India accepted the result, which removed an irritant and was followed by the 2015 land boundary agreement. Established Re-verify area figures
  • Risks: cyclones (Bulbul 2019, Amphan 2020), storm surge and rising salinity in the delta, human trafficking and smuggling across creeks, and illegal trawling.
  • Ports plan: deep-water port proposals such as Tajpur have repeatedly stalled, so Kolkata and Haldia remain the main gateways. Re-verify

Andaman and Nicobar Islands

The islands lie more than 1,000 km from the mainland and close to Myanmar, Thailand and Indonesia. Indira Point at the southern end of Great Nicobar is only about 150 km from Sumatra, and the chain sits near the western approach to the Strait of Malacca, through which a large share of Chinese energy imports passes. This geography explains much of their strategic value. The islands contribute roughly a third of India's EEZ. Re-verify

  • Command: the Andaman and Nicobar Command at Port Blair (2001) is India's only fully tri-service theatre-style command; its commander-in-chief rotates among the services. Naval bases include INS Utkrosh and INS Baaz (Campbell Bay, Great Nicobar), with airfields at Port Blair, Car Nicobar and elsewhere. Re-verify base names
  • Great Nicobar project: a large package of a transhipment port at Galathea Bay, airport, township and power plant, with a headline cost around ₹72,000 crore. Supporters stress strategic and economic value. Critics point to the Shompen and Nicobarese communities, forest loss, earthquake and tsunami risk and effects on leatherback turtles. Disputed
  • Protected peoples: the Sentinelese, Jarawa, Shompen and others are protected, and access to many areas is restricted by law.
  • Hazards: the 2004 earthquake and tsunami struck the islands hard, with damage to the Car Nicobar air base and the submergence of parts of Indira Point.
  • Diplomacy: the MILAN exercise was long hosted around Port Blair and later moved to Visakhapatnam.

Lakshadweep and small islands

Lakshadweep is a Union Territory of about 36 coral islands, atolls and reefs off Kerala, of which around ten are inhabited, with Kavaratti as its capital. Land area is tiny, but its waters enlarge India's EEZ and lie beside some of the busiest east-west shipping lanes in the world. The Eight Degree Channel between Minicoy and the Maldives and the Nine Degree Channel between Minicoy and the northern group are the key passages. Re-verify island counts

  • Naval presence: INS Dweeprakshak at Kavaratti (commissioned 2012) and INS Jatayu at Minicoy (commissioned 6 March 2024) give the Navy forward points close to those channels, alongside Coast Guard stations on the islands. Re-verify
  • Connectivity: a submarine optical-fibre cable from Kochi to the islands was developed to improve internet and governance reach. Re-verify commissioning
  • Politics: the islands drew attention in January 2024 after online exchanges with the Maldives over tourism, and earlier controversy over land and development regulations for the islands. Interpretation
  • Fragility: coral bleaching, erosion and sea-level rise threaten livelihoods; tuna fishing and coconut are the economic base.
Other small islands

Bet Dwarka (Gujarat), Sagar Island (West Bengal), Pamban/Rameswaram (Tamil Nadu) and the Odisha test islands each show how a small coastal patch can carry religious, military or ecological importance.

Part III · Security and strategy

How the coast is protected and what threatens it

Coastal security architecture after 26/11

On 26 November 2008, ten men trained and launched from Pakistan travelled by sea, hijacked the Indian fishing boat Kuber and landed at Mumbai in rubber boats, attacking several sites in the city over about three days. The lesson drawn was that coastal watch, intelligence and response had too many gaps. Established The reforms that followed are the core of this section.

Where the system is still tested

Tracking small wooden fishing boats is hard. Marine Police capacity, trained crews and maintenance differ widely between states, and shared data only helps if agencies act on it quickly. These are the points an examiner will want you to raise. Interpretation

Ports and strategic coastal assets

Around 95% of India's trade by volume, and a large share by value, moves by sea. Re-verify The government's Sagarmala programme (2015) and later maritime vision documents aim at port-led development, better hinterland links and more shipbuilding. A coast that matters this much economically has to be protected.

Ports

Twelve major ports have long been counted (Kandla/Deendayal, Mumbai, JNPT, Mormugao, New Mangalore, Cochin, Chennai, Kamarajar, V. O. Chidambaranar, Visakhapatnam, Paradip and Kolkata including Haldia), with Vadhavan planned as the next. Private ports such as Mundra and Pipavav handle large volumes. Re-verify

Shipyards

Defence yards include Mazagon Dock (Mumbai), Cochin Shipyard, Goa Shipyard, Garden Reach (Kolkata) and Hindustan Shipyard (Visakhapatnam), with private yards such as L&T Kattupalli. Shipbuilding is a strategic as well as an industrial asset.

Energy

Offshore fields (Mumbai High and east-coast basins), coastal refineries (Jamnagar, Mangaluru, Kochi, Paradip and Visakhapatnam), crude import terminals and LNG terminals (Dahej, Hazira, Kochi and others) sit on or near the shore.

Nuclear and data

Coastal nuclear plants (Tarapur, Kalpakkam, Kudankulam, with others planned) and the submarine cables that land at Mumbai, Chennai, Kochi and elsewhere make the coast a digital as well as a physical gateway. Interpretation of risk

A recurring exam point

Ports are also chokepoints in India's own economy. A short disruption at Mumbai-JNPT, Mundra or Chennai would affect supply chains across the country, which is why port security and cyber resilience belong in any coastal security answer.

Disaster response and humanitarian operations

The Navy and Coast Guard are first responders to coastal disasters as well as security forces. They provide ships, helicopters, divers and medical teams, while the National Disaster Response Force and state agencies run land operations. The Coast Guard also runs the national search and rescue system, with a main rescue coordination centre at Mumbai and sub-centres at other ports. Re-verify structure

Analysis, practice and revision

Perspectives, controversies and facts

Interpretation Is the coast safer than in 2008? One view points to radar chains, joint operations centres, registration and more boats and stations, with no repeat of 26/11. Another points to uneven state capacity, ageing patrol boats and the difficulty of tracking small craft. Both contain truth. Disputed

Interpretation Security versus livelihood. Tracking devices, registration and patrol rules help security but cost fishermen money and time. Palk Bay shows how a security boundary can collide with a living: the fishing ground and the line are the same place.

Interpretation Development versus ecology. Ports, the Great Nicobar project and coastal roads bring economic and strategic gains but threaten mangroves, turtle nesting sites, tribal communities and fragile islands. Coastal Regulation Zone rules attempt to balance the two, and each revision is contested.

Interpretation Unified maritime command. Supporters argue it would remove coordination gaps. Critics fear it could dilute service roles or tie assets to one region. No final design is public. Re-verify

Scenarios and strategic options

Presented neutrally, without recommending a political position:

Boat-borne attack on a port city

A small group lands from the sea near a dense city. Detection at 20-50 nm, rapid hand-off between Navy, Coast Guard and police, and local intelligence decide the outcome.

Cyclone near a port and a test range

A strong storm threatens Visakhapatnam or Odisha, with ships and missile ranges at risk, large evacuations and relief needs, and supply chains for the east coast disrupted.

Tension at the Kutch creeks

Boat incidents and boundary claims at Sir Creek test restraint, surveillance and the line between law enforcement and military action.

Disruption in the Eight Degree Channel

An incident on busy sea-lanes near Minicoy would affect shipping and energy flows and test forward bases and partner coordination.

Answer framework and worked example

For any regional or coastal security question, move through definition, location, evidence, contest, analysis, test, position, concession and close. One compressed example follows.

Worked example: "How well is India's coast protected against another 26/11?"

Define
Protection means detecting, identifying and stopping a hostile boat before and as it reaches the coast, and responding if it lands.
Locate
A multi-layer system running from the Navy offshore to the Coast Guard in territorial waters and the Marine Police close to shore, with joint operations centres at four sites.
Establish
Reforms since 2008 include coastal police stations, radar and AIS networks, fisher registration, the NC3I network and IFC-IOR, plus regular Sagar Kavach and Sea Vigil drills.
Contest
Small wooden boats remain hard to track, state capacity differs, and coastlines such as the creeks of Kutch and the Sundarbans are difficult to watch.
Analyse
The system is much stronger than in 2008, but it depends on intelligence, quick hand-offs and fisher cooperation as much as on hardware.
Test
Compare response times and detection records in exercises with real incidents, and compare states.
Position
Substantially improved but not complete; the weak points are small craft, uneven state capacity and coordination across agencies.
Concede
Public data on performance is limited, and some details are classified. Re-verify numbers
Close
The priority is to level up the inner layer and keep local communities as partners, not suspects.

Glossary

AIS
Automatic Identification System, a transponder that broadcasts a ship's identity and position.
Baseline
The line from which maritime zones are measured.
CRZ
Coastal Regulation Zone, rules that limit construction near the shoreline.
EEZ
Exclusive Economic Zone, up to 200 nautical miles from the baseline.
HADR
Humanitarian assistance and disaster relief.
IMBL
International Maritime Boundary Line.
IFC-IOR
Information Fusion Centre for the Indian Ocean Region, at Gurugram.
IUU fishing
Illegal, unreported and unregulated fishing.
JOC
Joint Operations Centre where agencies share a coastal picture.
Marine Police
State coastal police that patrol shallow waters.
MRCC
Maritime Rescue Coordination Centre.
NC3I
National Command, Control, Communication and Intelligence network.
Sagarmala
Programme for port-led development and coastal connectivity.
Territorial sea
Waters up to 12 nautical miles over which the state has sovereignty.

Rapid revision sheet

Framework

  • Coast about 7,516 km (traditional); 13 states and UTs; zones: 12 / 24 / 200 nm.
  • Layers: Navy (outer), Coast Guard (territorial waters), Marine Police (shallow waters).
  • Commands: Western (Mumbai), Eastern (Visakhapatnam), Southern (Kochi), A&N (Port Blair).

Regions

  • Gujarat: Kandla, Mundra, Sir Creek, Jamnagar, Coast Guard HQ NW at Gandhinagar.
  • Maharashtra and Goa: Mumbai, Mazagon Dock, JNPT, INS Hansa, Vadhavan.
  • Karnataka and Kerala: Karwar (Seabird), Kochi (Vikrant, Southern Command), Vizhinjam.
  • Tamil Nadu: Chennai, Tuticorin, Katchatheevu, Palk Bay, Kudankulam.
  • Andhra and Odisha: Visakhapatnam, SSBN base, Kalam Island, cyclones.
  • West Bengal: Kolkata, Haldia, GRSE, Sundarbans, 2014 award.
  • Islands: Andaman and Nicobar (Great Nicobar, Indira Point); Lakshadweep (INS Jatayu, Eight Degree Channel).

Security and strategy

  • 26/11 (2008), 1993 landings; JOCs at Mumbai, Visakhapatnam, Kochi, Port Blair; NC3I, IFC-IOR; Sagar Kavach, Sea Vigil.
  • Threats: terror, smuggling, illegal fishing, piracy, pollution, cyber, foreign survey ships.
  • Disasters: 2004 tsunami, Kerala floods, Fani, Tauktae; Coast Guard coordinates oil-spill response.
