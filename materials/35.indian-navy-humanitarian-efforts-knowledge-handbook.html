Part I · Foundations

Concepts, assets and early history

Snapshot and how to read

Humanitarian assistance and disaster relief (HADR) is now a routine task of the Indian Navy, listed in its doctrine alongside war fighting, diplomacy and constabulary duties. Ships reach coasts before roads and airfields do, carry their own food, water, power and medical care, and can stay for weeks. The pattern since 2004 is a Navy that moves fast to help India's neighbours and distant partners, and that is also used to bring Indians home from conflicts and disasters.

Why navies do humanitarian work

Three reasons usually stand out. First, a ship is a ready-made relief base: it has fuel, water, helicopters, divers, cooks, doctors and communications. Second, the sea often reaches places that land and air routes cannot, such as small islands or a damaged coast. Third, helping in a crisis builds trust and presence without the political weight of a military deployment. Interpretation

  • Law and legitimacy: relief to another state is normally offered at that state's request or with its consent, which separates it from intervention. A ship may also rescue persons in distress at sea under the duty of mariners and the SAR conventions, regardless of nationality.
  • Evacuation of nationals: a state's duty to protect its citizens abroad is the basis for NEOs. Evacuees are often also foreign nationals, which is a form of goodwill.
  • Doctrine: India's maritime doctrine and security strategy documents list benign roles such as humanitarian assistance and disaster relief alongside military and diplomatic roles. Re-verify document titles and years
  • Soft power: India presents itself as a "first responder" in the Indian Ocean Region, a term used by Indian officials and commentators. Interpretation
A useful distinction

Assistance (relief) differs from intervention (coercive action). The 1988 Maldives operation in section 4 shows why the line matters: India acted at the Maldivian government's request, which is why it is generally described as assistance rather than invasion.

Early history and doctrine

Before the 2000s the Navy's work in this field was real but irregular and rarely described as a mission in itself. Naval ships and helicopters supported relief after cyclones and floods on India's coast, such as the 1999 Odisha super cyclone and the 2001 Gujarat earthquake, though it was mainly the Army, Air Force and civil agencies that were visible in those operations. Re-verify naval roles

  • 1988, Maldives (Operation Cactus): mercenaries tried to overthrow the government in Malé. At the president's request, Indian paratroopers were airlifted in, and naval frigates intercepted the hijacked ship carrying the mercenaries. The operation is cited as a model of rapid assistance to a neighbour. Established
  • Neighbourhood context: Sri Lanka and the Maldives are small, close and dependent on the sea, and these relationships made naval assistance a natural tool.
  • Turning point: the response to the 2004 tsunami changed both the scale of the work and the way the Navy and government spoke about it.

How doctrine caught up

Indian maritime doctrine and strategy papers published from the mid-2000s onward formalised humanitarian roles and described them as part of the Navy's "benign" tasks. In the 2010s, mission-based deployments and regular HADR participation in exercises made the role routine. Re-verify dates

Part II · Case studies by type

Tsunami, evacuations, floods and relief missions

The 2004 tsunami

On 26 December 2004 an undersea earthquake off Sumatra generated a tsunami that killed more than 200,000 people around the Indian Ocean, including thousands on India's coast and in the Andaman and Nicobar Islands. India chose to manage its own relief and not seek foreign aid, while sending help to neighbours. The Navy was central to both.

Why 2004 matters beyond relief

During the response, the United States, India, Japan and Australia formed an informal "Tsunami Core Group" to coordinate relief. It is often cited as an early origin of the Quad. Interpretation of the link The wider Quad story is in other handbooks.

Evacuations I: Lebanon, Libya and Yemen

A naval evacuation needs a port, a reliable sea route, permission from local authorities and a safe window, and ships can carry many more people than aircraft can. Three operations illustrate how Indian practice developed.

  • Lebanon, 2006 (Operation Sukoon): during the Israel-Hezbollah war, naval ships including INS Mumbai, Brahmaputra and Betwa took people from Beirut by sea to Cyprus, from where they were flown home. The operation moved roughly two thousand people, Indians and nationals of other countries including Nepal and Sri Lanka. Re-verify ships and figures It is often described as the Navy's first major NEO.
  • Libya, 2011 (Operation Safe Homecoming): as the civil war began, India evacuated more than 15,000 people, mostly by air and by chartered ships, with Indian naval ships on station off the coast. Re-verify the naval share
  • Yemen, 2015 (Operation Raahat): during the conflict in Yemen, India brought out more than 4,500 Indians and several hundred foreign nationals of dozens of countries from Aden, Hodeidah and Mukalla by sea and air, with naval ships such as INS Sumitra, Mumbai and Tarkash and with Air India and Air Force aircraft. Re-verify numbers and ships
Goodwill by carrying foreigners

In Yemen and elsewhere, India also took foreign nationals on its ships and aircraft, and several governments publicly thanked it. That is cited as evidence of the soft-power dividend of evacuations. Interpretation

Evacuations II: COVID-19 and Sudan

Operation Samudra Setu (2020-21)

When international flights stopped in 2020, the Navy used amphibious and other ships, including INS Jalashwa and Magar, to bring Indians home from the Maldives, Sri Lanka and Iran, with strict health protocols. Several thousand people were repatriated in total. Re-verify the number In 2021 Samudra Setu II used ships to bring liquid medical oxygen and cylinders from the Gulf and Singapore during the second wave of the pandemic, filling a supply gap by sea. Re-verify quantities

Sudan (Operation Kaveri, 2023)

When fighting broke out in Sudan in April 2023, India evacuated its citizens through Port Sudan using naval ships such as INS Sumedha and INS Teg, together with Air Force transports, taking people across the Red Sea to Jeddah in Saudi Arabia and then home. Several thousand were moved. Re-verify

Not every evacuation was naval

Operation Ganga (Ukraine, 2022) was mainly run by air and land routes through neighbouring countries because of the Black Sea war zone, so the Navy had little part in it. Examiners may ask why some evacuations rely on ships and others on aircraft: the answer is access, distance, number of people and security conditions.

Floods and cyclones at home

At home the Navy is a supporting force: the National Disaster Response Force and state agencies lead, and naval boats, helicopters, divers and medical teams add capacity, especially on the coast and in large floods. More detail on coastal disasters is in handbook 34.

  • Chennai floods (2015): naval boats, helicopters and teams helped rescue people from flooded neighbourhoods. Re-verify
  • Kerala floods (2018): Operation Madad. Naval boats, helicopters, divers and aircraft rescued and supplied large numbers of people, and the ships at Kochi served as a base. Re-verify numbers
  • Cyclone Hudhud (2014): the Navy's own base at Visakhapatnam was hit, yet naval ships and teams supported the city's recovery.
  • Cyclone Fani (2019): naval ships and aircraft were put on standby on the east coast while Odisha carried out a large evacuation, showing prevention and pre-positioning.
  • Cyclone Tauktae (2021): naval ships and aircraft rescued crews of oil-field barges, including the barge P305 that sank off Mumbai with heavy loss of life, in difficult conditions. Re-verify figures
  • Cyclone Biparjoy (2023): naval and Coast Guard assets were readied on the Gujarat coast with large evacuations.
Prevention is also humanitarian

Improved cyclone warnings and pre-positioned assets are why recent cyclones caused far fewer deaths than the 1999 super cyclone. The Navy contributes mainly through readiness and rescue after landfall. Interpretation

Neighbourhood relief

India's closest neighbours by sea, Sri Lanka and the Maldives, are also the places where naval help is fastest and most visible. The Navy's presence in the Indian Ocean makes it a natural first call.

Maldives: Operation Neer (2014)

In December 2014 a fire at the water treatment plant in Malé left much of the capital without drinking water. India sent water by air and by sea. Naval ships, including INS Sukanya with desalination capacity and a tanker carrying fresh water, supplied Malé from the sea until repairs were complete. Re-verify ships and quantities The operation is a standard example of naval HADR meeting an immediate, life-critical need.

Sri Lanka

After the 2004 tsunami (Operation Rainbow) and again after floods and landslides in 2017, Indian ships and helicopters delivered food, medicine and supplies and carried medical teams. Re-verify 2017 ships During Sri Lanka's economic crisis in 2022, India's help was largely credit lines, fuel and food shipped commercially, with little naval role. That distinction matters in an answer: humanitarian help is not always naval.

Distant and recent relief missions

Since the mid-2010s the Navy has also carried relief to countries well beyond the neighbourhood, using ships that were already deployed or could reach the area in a few days.

  • Mozambique, 2019 (Operation Sahayata): after Cyclone Idai flooded much of Beira, naval ships including INS Sujata, Sarathi and Shardul delivered supplies, ran medical camps and rescued people from the floodwaters using boats and helicopters. Re-verify ships and numbers
  • Myanmar, 2023 onwards: after Cyclone Mocha (2023), Typhoon Yagi (2024) and the March 2025 earthquake (Operation Brahma), Indian Navy ships brought relief supplies to Yangon alongside air transport. Re-verify ships and operation names
  • Sri Lanka, 2025: following a severe cyclone, India launched a relief operation with naval and air assets. Re-verify details
  • Others: naval help has also gone to Indonesia, Tonga, Bangladesh and several African and island states at various times. Re-verify cases
Reading the geography

The destinations form a map of India's interests: the Indian Ocean littoral, Southeast Asia and East Africa. That is why analysts treat HADR as both humanitarian and strategic, and why the same ships that carry rice also carry flags. Interpretation

Medical support and health diplomacy

Medical care is one of the most valued naval contributions because it is quick to deploy and needed in almost every disaster. The Navy fits medical teams, supplies and sometimes field facilities onto amphibious and other ships, and runs medical camps during foreign deployments and port visits.

  • Disaster medicine: medical teams on ships and helicopters treat the injured, run triage and move casualties between ship and shore.
  • COVID-19: naval ships ferried oxygen in 2021 and medicines to partner countries in 2020 under Mission Sagar, and naval medical staff supported national efforts. India's vaccine diplomacy (Vaccine Maitri, 2021) was mainly delivered by air. Re-verify
  • Medical evacuation: naval helicopters and ships take seriously ill sailors off merchant and fishing vessels at sea.
  • Comparison: the United States (USNS Mercy and Comfort) and China (Peace Ark) operate dedicated hospital ships and use them in widely noted goodwill tours. India has no equivalent, which some analysts cite as a gap. Re-verify Indian plans

Hospital ship: a question for the exam

Arguments for: visible goodwill, dedicated capability and independence from ships needed for war. Arguments against: cost, a ship that is idle between crises, and the option of fitting medical modules on existing amphibious ships. Interpretation

Rescue at sea and merchant-ship protection

Search and rescue (SAR) at sea is a duty under international law and a daily task for the Coast Guard, with Navy support. India coordinates SAR over a very large ocean area through the main rescue coordination centre at Mumbai and regional centres. Re-verify area and structure Since 2008 the Navy has also escorted and protected merchant ships against piracy, and in recent years against missile and drone attacks.

  • Abhilash Tomy (2018): a naval officer taking part in a round-the-world race was injured in a storm in the southern Indian Ocean. Indian naval aircraft and ships joined the search, and he was recovered by a French vessel. Re-verify
  • Gulf of Aden and Arabian Sea, 2023-24: naval ships responded to attacks on merchant ships, for instance INS Visakhapatnam aiding the burning tanker Marlin Luanda in January 2024. Re-verify ship and date
  • MV Ruen (2024): after a ship hijacked by Somali pirates was boarded, INS Kolkata secured the surrender of the pirates and the release of the crew. Re-verify numbers
  • Fishing vessels: in early 2024 naval ships responded to hijacked Iranian and other fishing vessels, rescuing their crews, a good example of help to non-Indians. Re-verify

Escort and anti-piracy operations such as Operation Sankalp are covered in handbooks 1 and 23, and the legal background in handbook 32. The point here is that they are partly humanitarian: they protect seafarers of many nationalities.

Marine incidents and pollution

Oil spills, ship fires and groundings are environmental and humanitarian emergencies, and in India the Coast Guard is the lead agency and national coordinating authority for marine oil-spill response. The Navy supports with ships, helicopters, divers and firefighting where its assets are closest. Re-verify the exact arrangement

  • Ennore, Chennai (2017): two vessels collided off the port and spilled oil onto Chennai's beaches, with the Coast Guard leading the clean-up with state agencies.
  • Mauritius (2020): after the grounding and breakup of the bulk carrier Wakashio, India sent Coast Guard pollution-response equipment and experts by air. Re-verify
  • Sri Lanka (2021): after the chemical-laden ship X-Press Pearl caught fire and sank, India sent Coast Guard ships and help to fight the fire and the pollution. Re-verify
  • Kerala coast (2025): container-ship accidents off the coast led to a joint response and concern about spilled cargo and fuel. Re-verify
Exam point

If a question asks about the Navy's role in an oil spill, say that the Coast Guard leads under the national contingency plan and that the Navy supports. Mixing up the roles is a common error.

Part III · Strategy and critique

Diplomacy, partnerships and limits

HADR as diplomacy

Humanitarian work is one of the main ways India shows that its Navy serves the region, not only itself. It fits the doctrine of SAGAR (Security and Growth for All in the Region), announced in 2015, and its 2025 successor MAHASAGAR, which broadens the focus to the Global South. Both are covered in handbook 32.

  • Mission Sagar I (2020): during COVID-19, INS Kesari carried food, medicines and medical teams to the Maldives, Mauritius, Seychelles, Madagascar and Comoros. Re-verify
  • Mission Sagar II (2020): INS Airavat delivered food aid to Sudan, South Sudan, Djibouti and Eritrea. Later "Sagar" missions followed. Re-verify
  • Information sharing: the Information Fusion Centre for the Indian Ocean Region, opened in 2018, shares shipping information with partner navies, which supports rescue and security alike.
  • Neighbourhood First: the government's wider policy gives naval relief a political purpose, to show that India is the reliable neighbour.
  • Competition: other powers, notably China, also use hospital ships, port calls and relief as tools of influence, so India's record is partly a competition for goodwill. Interpretation
What "first responder" means

The phrase claims that India is usually the first state to reach a stricken neighbour, because of proximity, ships on station and speed of decision. It is a political description more than a legal status, and its accuracy differs between cases. Interpretation

Joint and multinational HADR

Large disasters need many actors, so navies train together for HADR. India takes part in several formats:

  • Quad: the United States, Japan, Australia and India include HADR in their cooperation. The grouping's roots are often traced to the 2004 tsunami response and its revival after 2017. Initiatives such as an Indo-Pacific partnership for maritime domain awareness also assist rescue as well as security. Re-verify names and dates
  • Exercises: HADR features in Malabar, in multinational exercises such as MILAN at Visakhapatnam, and in bilateral drills. Tiger Triumph, an India-US tri-service exercise, is built around HADR. Re-verify years
  • Regional bodies: the Indian Ocean Naval Symposium, which India helped found, runs working groups on HADR, and the Indian Ocean Rim Association addresses disaster-risk management. Re-verify
  • Resilience: India also leads the Coalition for Disaster Resilient Infrastructure, launched in 2019, which links disaster preparedness to development rather than to the Navy.

Detailed exercise lists are in handbook 23, and the Quad's structure in the Indo-Pacific handbooks. The humanitarian angle gives partnerships a non-confrontational face, which is useful for states that do not want to appear aligned against anyone.

Costs, limits and critiques

  • Capability gaps: few large amphibious ships, no hospital ship and an ageing landing-ship fleet limit how much India can carry or sustain, especially if two crises overlap. Interpretation
  • Competing demands: ships on relief are not on patrol. The Navy was also busy with anti-piracy and Red Sea deployments in 2023-24, showing how tasks compete.
  • Coordination: relief works best when it is requested, planned with local authorities and aligned with civil agencies. Offers that are not wanted can create friction.
  • Perception: neighbours may value the help while still fearing dependence. Some commentators treat HADR as a polite form of influence-seeking. Interpretation
  • Measurement: public numbers for rescues, tonnage and cost are patchy, so claims of success are hard to test. Re-verify any figure before use
  • Mission creep: the more relief the Navy does, the more it is expected to do, which may stretch an organisation whose first task is war fighting.
A balanced answer

Humanitarian work is a genuine good, a low-risk way to build goodwill and an opportunity to practise skills useful in war, such as logistics and amphibious operations. It is also limited by assets and does not replace deterrence. State both sides before choosing a position.

Perspectives, controversies and facts

Interpretation Soft power or strategy? One view treats naval HADR as simple duty to people in need. Another sees it as an instrument for access and influence in the Indian Ocean. Most analysts accept both motives. Disputed

Interpretation Should India build a hospital ship? Supporters point to China's and America's examples. Opponents point to cost and say modular medical facilities are more flexible.

Interpretation Help versus intervention. Relief at the host's request builds trust. Without consent it can look like pressure. The Maldives of 1988 and later cases are cited by both sides.

Interpretation Who should lead at home? The civil disaster agencies lead, and critics warn against turning every flood into a military mission, while supporters stress that the Navy's speed saves lives.

Lesser-known facts

  • Established India declined foreign aid in 2004 while sending ships to help neighbours.

  • Established The Coast Guard, not the Navy, leads marine oil-spill response.

Scenarios and strategic options

Presented neutrally, without recommending a political position:

Evacuation from a distant war

A conflict erupts near a port where many Indians live, as in Yemen or Sudan. Ships may be the only way out, and access, escorts and consent decide success.

Two disasters at once

A cyclone hits the east coast while a neighbour requests urgent help. Ship and helicopter numbers, crews and logistics are stretched.

A request in a politically tense country

A partner asks for help while its domestic politics are divided. Accepting or declining has diplomatic consequences, and local perception matters.

A major oil spill or ship fire close to shore

The Coast Guard leads and the Navy supports. Clear command, shared plans and equipment decide the outcome.

Answer framework and worked example

For any question on humanitarian operations, move through definition, location, evidence, contest, analysis, test, position, concession and close. One compressed example follows.

Worked example: "Is the Navy's humanitarian role a source of soft power or a distraction from war fighting?"

Define
HADR and NEO are relief and evacuation tasks; soft power is influence through attraction and goodwill.
Locate
Mostly the Indian Ocean Region, but also Southeast Asia, the Gulf, the Red Sea and East Africa.
Establish
Operations from the 2004 tsunami, Lebanon, Yemen, Operation Neer, Mozambique, Samudra Setu and Sudan show a repeated record of fast action and thanks from governments, including help to foreigners.
Contest
Critics say the ships and crews are not free, that capability gaps remain, and that goodwill does not become alliances.
Analyse
Many of the skills used (logistics, amphibious landings, command and control, medical support) are also war skills, so overlap is large. Conflict arises only when forces are stretched.
Test
Look at whether neighbours cooperated more with India after relief, and at readiness during simultaneous tasks.
Position
Mainly a source of soft power and practice, provided capacity is sized so that war fighting is not weakened.
Concede
Public data on costs and effect on neighbours is limited. Re-verify figures
Close
Invest in sealift and medical capability that serves both war and relief, and keep the Coast Guard and civil agencies in the lead at home.
