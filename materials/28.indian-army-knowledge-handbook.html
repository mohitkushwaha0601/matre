Snapshot and how to read

The Indian Army is the land component of India's armed forces and by far its largest service. It guards two live and disputed borders, runs the country's longest-running counter-insurgency commitments, and is a constant presence in disaster relief. It is also being re-shaped by drones, long-range fires and a new personnel model.

Part I · Foundations and structure

Where the Army came from and how it is built

Origins and the 1947 inheritance

The Indian Army descends from the presidency armies of the East India Company (Bengal, Madras, Bombay). After 1857 these were brought under the Crown, and in 1895 they were folded into a single Indian Army under four commands. Two world wars made it a large expeditionary force: over two million Indians served in the Second World War, the largest volunteer army in history. Re-verify the figure

Partition in 1947 divided the army, its units and its assets between India and Pakistan roughly in a two-to-one ratio. Interpretation Mixed-class regiments were split along religious lines, and the British officer cadre left in stages. The 1947 Tripartite Agreement among India, Nepal and the UK divided the Gorkha regiments: six went to India and four to the British Army. Established

  • 1895Presidency armies unified into the Indian Army with four commands.
  • 15 Jan 1949Lt Gen K.M. Cariappa takes over from Gen Sir Francis Butcher as the first Indian Commander-in-Chief. Army Day commemorates this.
  • 1955The title Commander-in-Chief is replaced by Chief of the Army Staff.
  • 1973 / 1986Manekshaw and Cariappa receive the ceremonial rank of Field Marshal.
The inheritance question

Much of the Army's regimental system, mess culture and training pattern is colonial in origin. Debates about recruitment (fixed-class versus all-India mix) and the Agnipath scheme are partly debates about how much of that inheritance to keep.

Civil-military framework and jointness

India follows civilian supremacy. The President is Supreme Commander, but authority runs through the Cabinet Committee on Security and the Defence Minister. The Ministry of Defence has four departments: Defence, Defence Production, Defence Research and Development, and Ex-Servicemen Welfare. Parliament oversees the budget and the Army Act.

Until 2019 the three Service Chiefs formed the Chiefs of Staff Committee with a rotating chairman. In January 2020 the post of Chief of Defence Staff (CDS) was created, with Gen Bipin Rawat as its first holder. The CDS is also Secretary of the new Department of Military Affairs (DMA) and permanent chairman of the Chiefs of Staff Committee. Gen Anil Chauhan was appointed in September 2022. Re-verify the current CDS

Existing tri-service commands

The Andaman and Nicobar Command (2001) and the Strategic Forces Command (2003) are the two joint commands. The Integrated Defence Staff supports the CDS.

Theatre commands (proposed)

Each theatre would unite Army, Navy and Air Force assets under a single commander. Public reporting speaks of three or four, for example a northern, a western and a maritime theatre. Re-verify The Air Force has long worried about dispersing a small fleet across theatres.

Organisation: commands to battalions

The Army is organised hierarchically: Army HQ, then Commands, Corps, Divisions, Brigades, Battalions (or regiments in armour and artillery), Companies and Platoons. A division has roughly 12,000-15,000 troops, a brigade 3,000-5,000 and an infantry battalion about 800-1,000. Interpretation: approximate

Reserve and paramilitary links. The Territorial Army (1949) is a part-time volunteer reserve. The Assam Rifles is administered by the Ministry of Home Affairs but operationally controlled by the Army, an overlap that periodically draws debate. Rashtriya Rifles (raised 1990) is the Army's dedicated counter-insurgency force in J&K.

Reforms under way

The Army has been moving from large, division-based formations to leaner, all-arms battle groups. It announced Rudra all-arms brigades, Bhairav light commando battalions, Shaktibaan and Divyastra artillery and drone units, and declared 2025 the "Year of Reforms". Re-verify the names and numbers

Arms, services, regiments and special forces

The Army separates arms (combat units) from services (support). Arms are Infantry, Mechanised Infantry, Armoured Corps, Artillery, Army Air Defence, Engineers, Signals and Army Aviation Corps. Services include the Army Service Corps, Army Ordnance Corps, Electronics and Mechanical Engineers, Army Medical Corps, the Remount and Veterinary Corps and the Judge Advocate General's branch.

The infantry is organised by regiment, each with its own regimental centre, traditions and recruiting areas: the Punjab, Rajputana Rifles, Maratha Light Infantry, Sikh, Dogra, Jat, Garhwal Rifles, Kumaon, Mahar, Grenadiers, the Gorkha Rifles, the Brigade of the Guards and the Parachute Regiment, among others. Armour carries older cavalry names such as the 61st Cavalry, one of the world's last horse-mounted units used chiefly for ceremony.

Special forces and the Agnipath question

The Para (SF) battalions are the Army's special forces, drawing on the Parachute Regiment. Infantry battalions also have "Ghatak" platoons. The Navy's MARCOS and the Air Force's Garud complete the tri-service special-forces set. The fixed-class composition of many units is at the centre of the Agnipath debate (see the people section).

What the Army has fought, where, and with what

Pattern to remember

From 1947 to 1971 the dominant theme was conventional war and civil-military mismatch; from 1984 to 1999 it was high altitude and limited war; since 2016 it has been calibrated retaliation under a nuclear ceiling.

Counter-insurgency and internal security

The Army has been in counter-insurgency roles almost continuously. In the North-East it began in Nagaland in the 1950s and extended to Mizoram, Manipur and Assam. In Jammu and Kashmir it intensified after 1989; the Rashtriya Rifles (1990) became its principal formation, and Operation All Out (2017) focused on militant leadership. The Army is not deployed against Left-Wing Extremism, which remains a police and central-armed-police task.

The legal framework: AFSPA

The Armed Forces (Special Powers) Act of 1958 gives the armed forces special powers in areas declared "disturbed". It has been reduced or withdrawn in parts of Tripura (2015), Meghalaya (2018) and, from 2022, many districts of Assam, Manipur and Nagaland. Re-verify the current map

Case for AFSPA

Soldiers need legal protection when operating against armed groups, and prosecutions without sanction would damage morale and operations.

Case against

Allegations of abuse and weak accountability fuel alienation. The 2005 Jeevan Reddy committee recommended repeal, and the Supreme Court in 2016 held that alleged excesses can be examined. Interpretation

Hearts and minds. Operation Sadbhavana (from 1998) runs schools, health camps and infrastructure projects in remote areas. Critics note that it supplements, but cannot replace, civil governance.

Borders, terrain and high altitude

The Army holds two different kinds of frontier. The Line of Control (about 740 km) with Pakistan is a ceasefire line fixed in 1972 under the Simla Agreement, where a February 2021 understanding revived the 2003 ceasefire. The Line of Actual Control with China is longer, about 3,488 km by the Indian count, and China claims a shorter figure, so the length itself is Disputed.

  • Siachen. The world's highest battlefield, held since Op Meghdoot (13 April 1984). Cold, avalanches and altitude kill more soldiers than fire. Posts sit above 5,000 m. The Actual Ground Position Line (AGPL) is not formally delimited.
  • Eastern Ladakh. The 2020 standoff ended in staged disengagements; an October 2024 patrolling arrangement covered Depsang and Demchok. Re-verify the current status
  • Arunachal and Sikkim. Dense forest and steep valleys favour defence in depth; Tawang, Doklam (2017) and Yangtse (2022) are recurring flashpoints.
  • Desert and plains. Mechanised warfare in Rajasthan and Punjab, shaped by canals and the intensive agricultural landscape.
Infrastructure is strategy

The Border Roads Organisation (1960, now under the Defence Ministry) builds roads and tunnels such as Atal Tunnel (2020), Sela Tunnel (2024) and the Darbuk-Shyok-DBO road. After 1962 the lesson was clear: logistics and forward roads determine how fast forces can be reinforced.

Doctrine and the two-front problem

India's nuclear doctrine (2003) sets no-first-use and credible minimum deterrence. Conventional doctrine therefore asks how the Army can act decisively below the nuclear threshold. After Operation Parakram (2001-02), when mobilisation took weeks, planners developed what is often called Cold Start, a limited and rapid offensive by smaller, ready formations. Officially its name has been neither fully embraced nor fully denied. Disputed

The Army Land Warfare Doctrine (2018) and the Joint Doctrine of the Indian Armed Forces (2017) stress jointness, speed and operations across domains. Pakistan's short-range "tactical" nuclear weapons and its stated first-use policy complicate any limited war. Established

The two-front problem

A collusive threat from China and Pakistan would force India to deploy against both. The late Gen Bipin Rawat used the phrase "two-and-a-half fronts", adding internal security as the half. Responses are layered: mountain strike corps for China, integrated battle groups for Pakistan, and strategic reserves.

Concepts examiners like

  • Integrated Battle Groups (IBGs): brigade-sized all-arms packages for rapid action.
  • Grey-zone and hybrid war: coercion short of declared war, including terror proxies, cyber and information operations.
  • "New normal" after Sindoor: the stated position that a major terror attack may invite a military response and that nuclear threats will not constrain it. Interpretation

Modernisation and Atmanirbharta

India has been among the world's largest arms importers, with Russia the largest supplier for decades. Re-verify the SIPRI shares Policy since 2020 aims to reverse this through Atmanirbhar Bharat: positive indigenisation lists, a "Buy (Indian-IDDM)" category in the Defence Acquisition Procedure, a defence-export drive and private-sector entry. The iDEX scheme (2018) funds start-ups. Emergency procurement powers were used after Galwan and Sindoor to field drones and ammunition quickly.

  • F-INSAS: the Future Infantry Soldier As A System programme, giving each soldier better weapons, protection, communications and night vision.
  • Technology bodies: the Army Design Bureau, the Army Cyber Group and the Defence Space Agency (2019).
  • Budget shape: the defence budget for 2025-26 was about ₹6.8 lakh crore. Re-verify Salaries and pensions consume a large share of the Army's own budget, leaving less for capital purchases. Interpretation
The structural problem

Indigenisation raises supply security, but delays in design, certification and testing mean imports and emergency buys persist. A good answer weighs capability today against self-reliance tomorrow.

Part III · People and roles

Soldiers, sustainment and the work beyond war

People, training and sustainment

The Army is an all-volunteer force with three tiers: commissioned officers, Junior Commissioned Officers (Naib Subedar, Subedar, Subedar Major) and other ranks. Officers are trained at the National Defence Academy (Khadakwasla), the Indian Military Academy (Dehradun, 1932) and the Officers Training Academies (Chennai and Gaya); the National Defence College and the Defence Services Staff College (Wellington) carry on higher education. The Army suffers a persistent officer shortfall. Re-verify the vacancy figure

Agnipath (2022)

Under Agnipath, recruits (Agniveers) serve four years, with about a quarter retained in the regular cadre after a selection process and the rest leaving with a Seva Nidhi lump sum. The government's stated aims are a younger force and lower long-term pension liability. Critics point to short tenures, training time, cohesion in regiments and the prospects of those who exit. Interpretation

  • Women in service: permanent commission across branches after the 2020 Supreme Court ruling; women are in the Military Police and are now admitted to the NDA. Colonel Sofiya Qureshi's briefings during Sindoor were a visible moment.
  • Gallantry awards: Param Vir Chakra (21 recipients since 1947), Ashoka Chakra, Maha Vir Chakra, Kirti Chakra, Vir Chakra and Shaurya Chakra.
  • Welfare: One Rank One Pension (2014-15), ECHS for medical care, and the Kendriya Sainik Board for resettlement.
Sustainment is a force multiplier

The Army Service Corps, Ordnance, EME and Medical Corps keep troops supplied at altitude, using mules, yaks and ponies in places roads do not reach. Ammunition stockpiles (War Wastage Reserves) have been an audit concern for years. Re-verify current levels

Beyond war: peacekeeping, exercises, relief

India has contributed more than 290,000 personnel to over 50 UN missions since 1950, and has long been among the top troop contributors. Re-verify the figures Current deployments include South Sudan, Lebanon (UNIFIL) and the Golan Heights, and the Army has provided commanders and women peacekeepers.

Bilateral and multilateral exercises

Yudh Abhyas (US), Dharma Guardian (Japan), Nomadic Elephant (Mongolia), Ajeya Warrior (UK), Shakti (France), Garuda Shakti (Indonesia), Mitra Shakti (Sri Lanka), Surya Kiran (Nepal), Ekuverin (Maldives) and others. They build interoperability and defence diplomacy.

Aid to the civil authority

Floods, earthquakes and cyclones: Op Surya Hope (Uttarakhand 2013), Op Madad (Kerala 2018), Bhuj (2001). Engineers build pontoon bridges, and the Army also supports mass events such as the Kumbh. The Army's own medical corps staffed a field hospital in Turkey in 2023 (Op Dost). Re-verify

The Army also runs training for the National Cadet Corps and operates schools and sports institutions that feed the Olympic movement. It is a lasting part of civic life, which is why public trust in it is high and why debates about its politicisation matter.

Analysis, practice and revision

Perspectives, controversies and facts

Interpretation Manpower versus technology. One school argues India must cut manpower and spend on drones, long-range fires and networks; the Shekatkar Committee (2016) proposed trimming non-combat posts. The opposing view says a long, mountainous, disputed frontier cannot be held by machines alone and that boots on the ground remain essential.

Interpretation Agnipath. Supporters see a younger, tech-ready force and lower pension costs. Critics see a loss of experience and regimental cohesion. Tenure, retention share and exit pathways remain under periodic review. Re-verify

Interpretation Theatre commands. Jointness would improve planning and resource use but raises questions about command authority, the role of the Air Force and the role of the Service Chiefs.

Interpretation Civil-military relations. India's civilian supremacy is strong, yet there is recurring debate over whether the military has enough voice in policy and whether public rhetoric around it has become too political. A professional Army stays apolitical by convention.

Lesser-known facts

  • Established In 1948 light tanks were disassembled and pulled up to Zojila at about 11,500 ft in secret, surprising the attackers.
  • Established Kargil produced four Param Vir Chakras; only two recipients, Yogendra Singh Yadav and Sanjay Kumar, lived to receive theirs.
  • Established Six Gorkha regiments came to India under the 1947 Tripartite Agreement; recruitment from Nepal has been in doubt since Agnipath began. Re-verify
  • Established The Army uses Zanskari ponies, yaks and Bactrian camels in the Ladakh region.

Scenarios and strategic options

Presented neutrally, without recommending a political position:

Managed deterrence

Border arrangements with China hold; the LoC ceasefire mostly holds; modernisation proceeds gradually.

Limited conflict with Pakistan

A terror attack triggers a short, high-tempo exchange of missiles, drones and artillery, with international pressure to de-escalate.

LAC friction

A local clash escalates in the mountains, testing forward logistics and surveillance and possibly opening a second front.

Technology-led transformation

Drones, counter-drone systems, networks and AI shift the balance, but only if procurement and training keep pace.

Answer framework and worked example

For any "Is X good for the Army?" question, move through definition, location, evidence, contest, analysis, test, position, concession and close. One compressed example follows.

Worked example: "Is Agnipath a net positive for the Army?"

Define
A four-year contractual entry scheme for non-officer ranks, with about 25% retention.
Locate
Launched June 2022 to reduce average age and pension cost.
Establish
Younger profile and lower long-term liabilities are real; so are shorter training and rapid turnover.
Contest
Critics cite cohesion and experience loss; supporters cite technology readiness.
Analyse
Modern units need trained specialists, which suits longer tenures; but the pool of fit young recruits is large.
Test
Examine retention rate, operational readiness, and post-exit outcomes.
Position
Net positive only if retention, training and exit pathways are calibrated.
Concede
Evidence is still limited because the first cohorts are only now completing tenure. Re-verify
Close
The principle is sound, the design is still being tested.
