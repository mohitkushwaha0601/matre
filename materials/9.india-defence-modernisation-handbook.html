Using this handbook

The short version: enough to speak accurately on defence modernisation and self-reliance, built to be read in two to three hours.

  • First pass: the snapshot and the revision sheet.
  • Second pass: Parts I-III in order.
  • Third pass: answer the practice questions aloud in two minutes each.
How to answer any defence question

What? → Why? → Background? → India's interest? → Security implication → Economic implication → argument for → argument against → what India should do. For thirty seconds, use the first, fourth and last steps only.

Claims are tagged Established Interpretation Disputed Re-verify. Defence figures change every year — check the latest budget and contracts before an interview.

The idea in one line

Self-reliance does not mean making everything at home. It means controlling the capabilities that could be denied to you in a crisis.

Part I

Foundations

Lessons of war, 1947-1999

Every war taught India something about its equipment and organisation. Not every lesson was acted on.

Wars and what they taught
Event Lesson
1947-48 A small, British-equipped force; ordnance factories but no design capacity. DRDO was created in 1958.
1962 Unprepared for high-altitude war. Rapid expansion followed, and a turn to Soviet weapons (MiG-21) that lasted four decades.
1965 · 1971 Soviet partnership and better planning paid off; 1971 was a model of joint operations under strong leadership.
1974 · 1983 Pokhran-I; the Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme (1983) and the Light Combat Aircraft programme.
1987 Bofors scandal: procurement froze for years, creating a culture of caution.
1998 Pokhran-II; the BrahMos agreement with Russia.
1999 Kargil exposed gaps in surveillance, equipment and jointness — the start of modern reform.

A fact that wins arguments. Established The Bofors gun performed well in Kargil. The scandal was about the contract, not the weapon.

Higher defence reform and jointness

  • 1999-2001Kargil Review Committee and the Group of Ministers: HQ Integrated Defence Staff, the Andaman & Nicobar Command, the Defence Intelligence Agency, and the Strategic Forces Command (2003).
  • 2012 · 2016Naresh Chandra Task Force; Shekatkar Committee on combat efficiency.
  • 1 Jan 2020Chief of Defence Staff and Department of Military Affairs created.
  • 2021Ordnance Factory Board corporatised into seven public-sector companies (1 October).
  • 2023Inter-Services Organisations Act gives commanders authority over personnel of all three services in joint units.

Theatre commands

India has 17 single-service commands. The plan is to merge them into a few integrated theatre commands (for example, facing China, facing Pakistan, and maritime). The Andaman & Nicobar Command remains the only full tri-service theatre command. Re-verify

Why it is slow

Interpretation Services worry about losing control of scarce assets — especially the Air Force, with too few squadrons to divide among theatres. Jointness is a matter of culture and trust as much as organisation charts.

Budget and manpower

Where the money goes

  • Salaries and pensions take the largest share
  • Capital (new equipment) is roughly a quarter
  • Most capital goes on paying for past contracts, leaving little for new ones
  • About 75% of capital acquisition is reserved for Indian industry Re-verify

Manpower

  • Around 1.4 million active personnel, mostly Army
  • Agnipath (June 2022): four-year tenure for soldiers, up to 25% retained
  • Aims: younger forces, lower pension bills
  • Concerns: training time, unit cohesion, re-employment
The real problem

India spends around 2% of GDP on defence, but a large force and pensions leave a small capital budget. Many analysts argue for 2.5% of GDP or a leaner force; both are politically hard.

Part II

The forces

The Army

Key equipment
Area Systems
Artillery Bofors FH-77B; M777 ultra-light howitzers (US); K9 Vajra (Korean design, built by L&T); Dhanush; ATAGS; Pinaka rocket system.
Armour T-72 and T-90 tanks; Arjun Mk1A; Zorawar light tank for the mountains (under trial).
Infantry INSAS (1998) being replaced; AK-203 made in Amethi (Indo-Russian JV); SIG716 rifles.
Aviation Dhruv ALH, Rudra, Prachand light combat helicopter, Apache.

Focus since 2020: shift of forces towards the China front, high-altitude equipment, roads and tunnels, drones, loitering munitions and integrated battle groups.

The artillery story. After Bofors, India did not buy a new towed gun for almost 30 years. The M777 (2016) and K9 Vajra ended that gap; ATAGS and Dhanush are the Indian answer.

The Air Force

The Air Force's biggest problem is numbers: it is sanctioned 42 fighter squadrons but has around 30 or fewer. Re-verify

Fighter fleet
Aircraft Origin Note
Su-30MKI Russia (licence-built by HAL) The backbone of the fleet.
Rafale France 36 bought via a 2016 government-to-government deal.
Tejas Mk1 / Mk1A India 83 Mk1A ordered in 2021, more later; deliveries delayed by US engine supply. Re-verify
Mirage 2000, MiG-29, Jaguar Various Ageing but upgraded; MiG-21s retired.
AMCA India Fifth-generation fighter, approved 2024; years from service.

Support aircraft: AEW&C (Netra), refuellers (a shortage), C-17 and C-130J transports, and the C-295 — the first military aircraft to be built by a private Indian company (Tata, at Vadodara).

The Navy

The Navy is the most indigenised service — a “builder's navy”. Most of its ships on order are being built in India.

  • Carriers: INS Vikramaditya (Russian, 2013) and INS Vikrant (Indian-built, commissioned 2 September 2022). Rafale-M ordered for carrier use in 2025. Re-verify
  • Destroyers and frigates: Kolkata- and Visakhapatnam-class destroyers; Nilgiri-class frigates.
  • Submarines: six Kalvari-class (French Scorpene, built at Mazagon); ageing Russian and German boats; the P-75(I) project for new submarines with air-independent propulsion.
  • Nuclear submarines: INS Arihant (2016) and INS Arighaat (2024) carry nuclear missiles.
  • Maritime patrol: P-8I aircraft, MH-60R helicopters, MQ-9B drones.
Why the Navy matters more now

Interpretation China's navy is the world's largest and is increasingly present in the Indian Ocean. India's advantage is geography — it sits astride the sea lanes. But submarine numbers are a serious gap.

Strategic forces and missiles

India's greatest defence R&D success: its missiles, warheads and nuclear submarines are indigenous.

  • Doctrine (2003): credible minimum deterrence, No First Use, massive retaliation, civilian control through the Nuclear Command Authority.
  • Triad: land (Agni series), air (fighter-delivered) and sea (Arihant-class submarines). Complete since INS Arihant's first deterrence patrol in 2018.
  • Agni-V with multiple warheads (MIRV) tested in March 2024 (Mission Divyastra).
  • BrahMos: Indo-Russian supersonic cruise missile; exported to the Philippines (2022).
  • Mission Shakti (2019): anti-satellite missile test.
Why this worked

Interpretation Strategic programmes had priority, secrecy, stable funding and continuous leadership — exactly what many conventional programmes lacked. That is the model for the rest.

Air defence and drones

Layers of air defence
Layer Systems
Long range S-400 (Russia, 2018 contract; deliveries continuing) Re-verify
Medium range MRSAM (with Israel); Akash (Indian)
Short range QRSAM, guns, shoulder-fired missiles
Ballistic missile defence DRDO's two-layer programme
Counter-drone Jammers, lasers and guns; integrated networks

Drones: India uses Heron (Israel) and MQ-9B (US, contract October 2024) for surveillance, and Indian loitering munitions. The first drone attack on an Indian base was at Jammu air station in June 2021.

Re-verify In August 2025 the government announced Mission Sudarshan Chakra, a national air-defence shield. Check its progress.

The cost problem. A drone may cost a few lakh rupees; a missile to shoot it down may cost crores. Cheap defences — guns, jammers, lasers — are essential.

Space, cyber, ISR and logistics

Space

Defence Space Agency (2019); military satellites (GSAT-7 for the Navy, GSAT-7A for the Air Force); a large surveillance satellite constellation planned. Re-verify

Cyber

Defence Cyber Agency (2019). Cyber and electronic warfare are now part of every conflict.

ISR

Intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance: satellites, drones, AEW&C aircraft and maritime patrol. The lesson of Kargil was that you cannot fight what you cannot see.

Logistics

Agreements with the US (LEMOA, 2016) and others give access to partner bases for fuel and supplies. Ammunition stocks for sustained war remain a concern.

Part III

Atmanirbhar defence

How India buys weapons

The process: the service states a need → Acceptance of Necessity (AoN) from the Defence Acquisition Council → trials → the lowest compliant bidder (L1) is selected → Cabinet Committee on Security approval for big deals → contract. Headlines saying “₹X crore approved” usually mean AoN, not a contract.

DAP 2020 categories, in order of preference
Category Meaning
Buy (Indian-IDDM) Indigenously designed, developed and manufactured
Buy (Indian) From an Indian vendor with minimum indigenous content
Buy and Make (Indian) Indian firm with foreign technology
Buy (Global - Manufacture in India) Foreign vendor producing in India
Buy (Global) Direct import — the last option

Why it is slow

  • Over-ambitious or changing requirements
  • Fear of scandals after Bofors (1987), Tehelka (2001) and AgustaWestland (2013)
  • Long trials; the lowest-price rule over lifecycle value
  • The 126-fighter MMRCA competition (2007) was cancelled in 2015 after years of negotiation

Offsets — requiring foreign vendors to invest part of the contract in India — were largely dropped in DAP 2020 for government-to-government deals after poor results.

The defence-industrial ecosystem

DRDO

About 50 laboratories; designs missiles, radars, electronics, aircraft and armour.

Public sector

HAL, BEL, BDL, Mazagon Dock, GRSE, Goa Shipyard, BEML and seven ex-Ordnance Factory companies.

Private sector

L&T, Tata, Bharat Forge, Adani, Solar Industries and thousands of small firms and startups.

The policy tools

  • Positive Indigenisation Lists (from August 2020): items that can no longer be imported after a set date.
  • iDEX (2018): funds startups to solve defence problems.
  • Defence corridors in Uttar Pradesh and Tamil Nadu (2018).
  • FDI up to 74% through the automatic route (2020).
  • Budget reservation of most capital acquisition for domestic industry.
  • Exports: BrahMos to the Philippines; Pinaka and Akash to Armenia; ammunition and components to many countries.
Content is not the same as design

Interpretation A product can be “70% indigenous” by cost while its engine or chip — the part that can be denied — is imported. Real self-reliance means owning the design and the critical components.

Failures, successes, chokepoints

Four hard cases
Programme Started What happened
Tejas 1983 First flight 2001; squadron 2016. Slow, but it now flies and is in production.
Arjun tank 1974 Inducted in small numbers; heavy for some terrain; limited orders.
INSAS rifle 1998 Reliability complaints; being replaced.
Kaveri engine 1989 Did not reach fighter-engine standard; Tejas uses a US engine.

Successes: Agni and Prithvi missiles, BrahMos, Akash, Pinaka, Dhruv helicopter, warships and the Arihant submarines.

Chokepoints: jet and marine engines, advanced semiconductors, sensors, special alloys and rare-earth magnets (largely controlled by China), plus spares for Russian equipment.

Make or buy?

Buy what is urgent, make what is critical, and co-develop what you cannot yet make alone — with real technology transfer. Every major arms producer had failures on the way.

Lessons from Ukraine and Sindoor

Ukraine (2022-)

  • Long wars need ammunition and industrial depth
  • Cheap drones change the battlefield
  • Electronic warfare decides whether drones work
  • Dependence on a single supplier is a risk (India's Russian spares)

Operation Sindoor (May 2025)

  • Precision strikes at long range
  • Layered air defence against drones and missiles
  • Indian systems — BrahMos, Akash, counter-drone networks — reported in use
  • Disputed Claims of losses and damage on both sides are contested
The future

AI-enabled targeting, drone swarms, hypersonic missiles, space and cyber. The winner will be the side that can produce, adapt and replace fastest — which is the real case for Atmanirbharta.

Part IV

Knowledge toolkit

Views, controversies, lesser-known facts

Schools of thought
School Argument
Indigenisation first Accept delays to build a domestic base; imports create lasting dependence.
Capability first Buy the best available now; threats will not wait for Indian industry.
Partnership Co-develop with trusted partners (US, France, Israel) to gain technology faster.

Controversies

  • Rafale deal (2016): political allegations over price and offset partner. The Supreme Court dismissed petitions in 2018 and 2019. Disputed politically.
  • Agnipath: younger forces and lower pensions versus shorter training and cohesion concerns.
  • Public vs private: should the public sector keep its dominant role?

Lesser-known facts

  • HAL's HF-24 Marut (1961) was India's first indigenous jet fighter — designed with German help, limited by its engine.
  • The Navy set up its own design directorate in the 1960s — one reason it is the most indigenous service.
  • India's defence exports now reach many countries, including the US for components.

Scenarios and options

Steady progress

Production and exports keep rising; engines and chips remain imported.

Breakthrough

Engine co-production and AMCA succeed; India becomes a major arms exporter.

Crisis test

A two-front crisis exposes shortages of fighters and ammunition.

Supply shock

Sanctions or export curbs (Russian spares, Chinese rare earths) disrupt production.

What India should do

  • Structures: complete theatre commands; faster, simpler procurement.
  • Money: protect the capital budget; fund R&D steadily.
  • Industry: give the private sector a fair chance; long-term orders; testing facilities for all.
  • Technology: focus on engines, chips, sensors, drones and ammunition.
  • Partners: co-develop with trusted partners with real technology transfer.

Glossary

Atmanirbhar Bharat
“Self-reliant India” — the 2020 policy framework, applied to defence.
CDS / DMA
Chief of Defence Staff / Department of Military Affairs (2020).
DAP
Defence Acquisition Procedure 2020.
AoN
Acceptance of Necessity — first approval of a need; not a contract.
IDDM
Indigenously Designed, Developed and Manufactured — the top procurement category.
DPSU
Defence Public Sector Undertaking, e.g. HAL, BEL, Mazagon Dock.
iDEX
Innovations for Defence Excellence — support for startups (2018).
Positive Indigenisation List
Items that may no longer be imported after a set date.
Offsets
A foreign vendor's obligation to invest part of the contract in India.
ToT
Transfer of Technology — often a licence to build, not design know-how.
Theatre command
One commander with forces from all three services for a region.
Squadron
The basic IAF unit, usually 16-18 aircraft.
SSBN / SSK
Nuclear-powered ballistic missile submarine / diesel-electric submarine.
MIRV
Several warheads on one missile, each aimed at a different target.
ISR
Intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance.
Loitering munition
A drone that circles an area and dives onto a target.

Ready answers

  • What is modernisation? The ability to find, decide and strike — sustained over time, used jointly.
  • What is Atmanirbharta? Controlling what could be denied in a crisis; buying the rest on terms that build Indian capability.
  • Biggest gap? Pick one — fighters, engines or jointness — and say how to close it.
  • What should India do? Fix structures, fund essentials, build what matters, buy what is urgent.
