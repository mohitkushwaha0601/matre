Indian Defence Exercises 2026

A research compendium of major exercises and operational training engagements involving the Indian Army, Navy, Air Force, Coast Guard and joint/multi-agency formations, covering events completed or underway by 28 September 2026.

Cut-off: 28 Sep 2026 Primary evidence: PIB / MoD Scope: major publicly documented exercises Neutral analysis • no political ranking
Method note: “All” is interpreted as all major, publicly documented exercises / structured training engagements that could be verified from official Indian defence releases and selected government/major-media sources through the cut-off date. Routine unit drills, ship-to-ship passage exercises, ceremonial displays and every state-level recurring coastal-security iteration are not exhaustively listed. Where official 2026 confirmation was not available, an item is not presented as verified.

2026 at a glance

The exercise calendar shows several overlapping themes: greater jointness inside India, deeper Indo-Pacific interoperability, expanded use of drones / counter-UAS / ISR, special-operations interoperability, and a strong maritime-security layer built around the Indian Ocean Region.

Strategic pattern map

1 — Jointness

Lamitiye, Sanyukt Kavach, MILAN and Dustlik show India training beyond single-service silos: command relationships, air-maritime links, civil-military response and combined planning are recurring themes.

2 — Technology

Drones, counter-UAS, ISR, autonomous systems, networked C2 and air refuelling appear repeatedly—from Army tactical drills to IAF and maritime exercises.

3 — Indo-Pacific

MILAN, IONS IMEX, Southern Readiness, ADMM-Plus, Pitch Black, Udara Shakti, SLINEX and the QUAD coast-guard mission show a layered approach to maritime and air cooperation.

4 — Special operations

Khanjar, Vajra Prahar and Cyclone emphasise small-team interoperability, mission planning and terrain-specific TTPs.

5 — Neighbourhood first

PRAGATI, SLINEX, Maitree and Lamitiye show emphasis on training relationships with Indian Ocean and Southeast Asian partners.

6 — Expeditionary credibility

Pitch Black and overseas bilateral exercises test the ability to deploy aircraft, crews, sustainment and procedures beyond home bases.

SSB / Interview lens

What happened? India’s 2026 exercise portfolio combined combat-readiness drills, special-forces training, multinational air/naval exercises and military-civil preparedness.

Why it matters? Exercises convert defence relationships into rehearsed procedures: communication, command-and-control, logistics, interoperability, humanitarian response and shared TTPs.

Competing viewpoints to understand: A large or frequent exercise can be read as confidence-building and interoperability-building; observers can also question whether participation produces meaningful operational integration or mainly signalling. The evidence to test that distinction is the complexity of the drills, repeatability, common procedures, data-sharing, logistics, and whether training outputs are carried into doctrine and procurement.

Good SSB framing: “The value of a military exercise should be judged by the interoperability problem it rehearses—rather than by the name, size or media visibility of the exercise.”

Source discipline & gaps

Official Indian government releases are the primary evidence base. Secondary professional sources are used only where an exercise was publicly documented but a directly searchable PIB release was not located. Several annual exercises normally associated with India—such as some Army cycles with Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Australia, Singapore or France—do not appear in this verified set unless a 2026 edition could be confirmed by the cut-off. Their absence from this page should not be read as proof that no training interaction occurred; it means the exercise was not included as a verified major 2026 event from the accessible public record used here.
