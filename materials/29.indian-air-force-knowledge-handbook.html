Snapshot and how to read

The Indian Air Force (IAF) is the air arm of India's armed forces. It provides air defence, strike, reconnaissance, airlift and helicopter support for the Army and Navy, and it carries the air-delivered part of India's deterrence. Its central public debate is whether it has enough fighter squadrons to face two adversaries at once.

Part I · Foundations and structure

Where the IAF came from and how it is built

Origins and growth

The Indian Air Force was established on 8 October 1932 by the Indian Air Force Act. Its first flight was formed on 1 April 1933 at Drigh Road, Karachi, with Westland Wapiti biplanes, six Indian officers and 19 Indian airmen ("Hawai Sepoys"). Established In the Second World War the service fought in the Burma campaign. It received the "Royal" prefix in 1945 and dropped it when India became a republic in 1950.

At Partition in 1947 the assets of the Royal Indian Air Force were divided with Pakistan. The IAF was first used within weeks: on 27 October 1947 it airlifted troops to Srinagar after Kashmir's accession. Its first Indian chief was Air Marshal Subroto Mukerjee (1954). Established

From British designs to a mixed fleet

Early fleets were British (Vampire, Hunter, Canberra) and later Soviet-origin (MiG-21 from 1963, Su-30MKI from 2002). The IAF has since added French and American aircraft, which is why its fleet and its spares chain are among the most diverse in the world.

Civil-military framework and jointness

As with the Army, India follows civilian supremacy. The Air Chief reports to the Defence Minister, and the Ministry of Defence's four departments (Defence, Defence Production, Defence Research and Development, and Ex-Servicemen Welfare) control policy and money. The Chief of Defence Staff (CDS), created in 2020, is Permanent Chairman of the Chiefs of Staff Committee and heads the Department of Military Affairs.

Two joint commands exist: Andaman and Nicobar Command (2001) and Strategic Forces Command (2003). Proposed theatre commands would place Army, Navy and Air Force assets under one commander for each geographic theatre. Re-verify the current status

The IAF's concern

Air power is flexible and scarce. Splitting it among theatres could reduce the number of aircraft available for any single decisive effort, and a theatre commander who is not an airman might use it for close support rather than strategic effect. Interpretation

The case for theatres

Unified planning and command would remove duplication, speed up decisions and make joint operations routine. Supporters argue that air assets can still be pooled through an air-defence command.

Bases that recur in exam answers

Rafale squadrons sit at Ambala (17 Squadron "Golden Arrows") and Hasimara (101 Squadron "Falcons"); Tejas Mk1 at Sulur; Su-30MKI at several eastern and western bases including Tezpur, Chabua, Halwara and Bareilly. Re-verify basing

Branches, ranks and special units

Officers belong to the Flying, Technical or Ground Duties branches. Flying includes fighter, transport and helicopter streams. Ground Duties covers administration, logistics, accounts, education, meteorology and the air-traffic and fighter-control trades.

Officer ranks run from Pilot Officer, Flying Officer, Flight Lieutenant, Squadron Leader, Wing Commander, Group Captain and Air Commodore to Air Vice Marshal, Air Marshal and Air Chief Marshal, with Marshal of the IAF as the ceremonial five-star rank. Airmen ranks include Aircraftman, Leading Aircraftman, Corporal, Sergeant, Junior Warrant Officer, Warrant Officer and Master Warrant Officer.

One Param Vir Chakra

Flying Officer Nirmal Jit Singh Sekhon, who flew a Gnat in defence of Srinagar airfield in December 1971, is the only IAF recipient of the Param Vir Chakra, awarded posthumously.

What the IAF has done, which aircraft it flies, and how it plans

Pattern to remember

From 1947 to 1971 the IAF's role grew from airlift to decisive support; from 1999 it became a tool of calibrated, politically limited force; since 2019 its cross-border strikes have been openly acknowledged.

Fighter fleet and squadron strength

The IAF's sanctioned strength is 42 fighter squadrons. Retirements of older aircraft, including the final MiG-21 squadrons in 2025, have taken the serving figure to roughly 29-31. Re-verify The shortfall is the main public worry about the service.

The MiG-21 served from 1963 and was long criticised for its accident record, sometimes called a "flying coffin", though its role in 1971 and in the 2019 air combat was also significant. Its retirement closed a sixty-year chapter.

Quantity versus quality

Counting squadrons alone is misleading: a squadron with AEW&C cover, mid-air refuelling, long-range missiles and electronic warfare can do more than a larger force without them. Examiners reward answers that mention both numbers and force multipliers.

Airlift, helicopters and force multipliers

  • Strategic and tactical airlift: C-17 Globemaster III (11 aircraft), C-130J Super Hercules (12), IL-76 and An-32. The Tata-Airbus C-295 (56 ordered, 40 to be built in India) is replacing the Avro fleet.
  • Helicopters: Mi-17 V5 (the workhorse), CH-47F Chinook for heavy lift, AH-64E Apache and the indigenous LCH Prachand for attack, ALH Dhruv for utility.
  • Airborne early warning: Netra AEW&C (Embraer-based) and the A-50 Phalcon; more AEW&C aircraft are planned.
  • Mid-air refuelling: IL-78 tankers; the small tanker fleet limits range and endurance.
  • Unmanned and training: Heron UAVs; 31 MQ-9B drones were contracted in 2024 for the three services; training uses the Hawk, PC-7 Mk II and the indigenous HTT-40. Re-verify numbers
Why force multipliers matter

A fighter's radar sees little; an AEW&C aircraft sees a theatre. A tanker extends reach and loiter time. Both multiply the effect of every fighter, which is why shortages in these fleets matter as much as shortages in squadrons.

Air defence and missiles

Air defence is a layered system: fighters at long range, long- and medium-range surface-to-air missiles (SAMs) in the middle, and short-range missiles and guns close to targets. The IAF operates the S-400 Triumf (contracted from Russia in 2018 for five squadrons, with delivery delays for the later units), the Akash, SPYDER and MRSAM/Barak-8 systems. The Army also operates SAMs and gun systems, so the two services must be tied by a common air picture, the Integrated Air Command and Control System. Re-verify the S-400 delivery status

Air-launched weapons include BrahMos-A on the Su-30MKI, the indigenous Astra beyond-visual-range missile, Meteor and SCALP on the Rafale, and the Rudram anti-radiation missile in development. In August 2025 the Prime Minister announced Mission Sudarshan Chakra, a national air-defence shield planned for around 2035. Re-verify the scope

Concepts examiners like

  • Layered defence: no single system is enough; overlap is the point.
  • Counter-drone: cheap drones force expensive interceptors; sensors, guns and electronic jamming are being added.
  • Stand-off weapons: striking from outside enemy air defences lowers pilot risk but raises cost per sortie.

Doctrine and the two-front problem

The IAF's basic doctrine describes roles: counter-air (including suppression of enemy air defences), strategic air operations, battlefield air interdiction, close air support, reconnaissance and airlift. It stresses that air power is indivisible and should be commanded centrally. The doctrine sits below India's declared policy of no first use of nuclear weapons.

India plans for a collusive threat from China and Pakistan. At 42 sanctioned squadrons the IAF's own planners have long argued that it could fight a two-front war only with some risk, and current strength is lower still. Interpretation Part of the answer is to rotate aircraft between fronts quickly, using bases in depth and mid-air refuelling; part is to rely on stand-off weapons and air defence.

Air power in a limited war

Since 2016 the political leadership has accepted limited cross-border strikes as an option. The IAF's task is to deliver such strikes without triggering unmanageable escalation, which requires accurate weapons, electronic warfare and a credible air-defence umbrella over Indian cities and bases.

Modernisation and Atmanirbharta

Modernisation has four threads. First, the Tejas Mk1A, built by HAL with a General Electric F404 engine; a combined order of about 180 aircraft is meant to replace the MiG-21 and other older types. Second, the Advanced Medium Combat Aircraft (AMCA), a planned fifth-generation stealth fighter, with induction hoped for in the 2030s. Re-verify timelines Third, a larger imported or jointly built fighter purchase, widely reported as around 114 Medium Multi-Role Fighter Aircraft, with the Rafale discussed as a leading option. Re-verify the status Fourth, engines: the GE F414 co-production deal with HAL and the long-delayed Kaveri programme.

  • Unmanned systems: "loyal wingman" concepts (HAL's CATS Warrior), swarming drones and high-altitude long-endurance UAVs.
  • Space and cyber: the Defence Space Agency (2019) and the Defence Cyber Agency (2019); the IAF has argued for an aerospace role.
  • Policy tools: positive indigenisation lists, the Buy (Indian-IDDM) category, iDEX start-ups and emergency procurement.
The structural problem

Indigenous programmes are essential for long-term self-reliance, but their delays mean the IAF must also buy foreign aircraft now. A good answer weighs capability today against self-reliance tomorrow, and notes that engines and avionics are the hardest links in the chain.

Part III · People and roles

Airmen, sustainment and the work beyond war

People, training and sustainment

Officers enter through the National Defence Academy (Khadakwasla), the Air Force Common Admission Test (AFCAT) or the Combined Defence Services exam, and train at the Air Force Academy, Dundigal (Hyderabad). Flying training moves from basic trainers to the Hawk advanced jet trainer before pilots are posted to an operational type. Airmen are recruited for technical and non-technical trades and, since 2022, through the Agniveervayu route for four-year terms. Interpretation: the Agnipath debate applies here too, especially for technical trades that need long training

  • Women in the IAF: women were admitted to the fighter stream from 2016, when Avani Chaturvedi, Mohana Singh and Bhawana Kanth were commissioned as the first three fighter pilots; women also fly transport aircraft and helicopters and serve in operational command roles.
  • Welfare and honours: One Rank One Pension, the ECHS medical scheme, and gallantry awards from Vayu Sena Medal to Param Vir Chakra.
  • Pilots' working life: fighter flying demands strict medical and age standards; the service has dealt with pilot and spares shortages and trainer-aircraft ageing. Re-verify
Sustainment decides readiness

Serviceability is not the same as the number of aircraft owned. Maintenance Command, base repair depots, HAL and spares from several countries keep the fleet flying. Diverse fleets and import dependence make this chain long and fragile, and sanctions or supply disruptions can ground aircraft.

Beyond war: relief, evacuation, exercises

The IAF is the first responder in many disasters. In Uttarakhand in 2013 it ran Operation Rahat, a very large helicopter rescue in which an IAF Mi-17 crashed with 20 aboard. Re-verify the casualty count It lifts relief supplies after floods and earthquakes, and its Chinooks and Mi-17s fight forest fires and move disaster forces.

Evacuations and aid flights

Op Devi Shakti (Afghanistan, 2021), Op Ganga (Ukraine, 2022), Op Kaveri (Sudan, 2023) and Op Dost (earthquake relief for Turkey and Syria, 2023). Large transports such as the C-17 and C-130J are the backbone.

Exercises and engagement

Tarang Shakti (India-hosted multinational), Red Flag Alaska (US), Garuda (France), Pitch Black (Australia), Eastern Bridge (Oman) and Cope India. They build interoperability and expose pilots to different tactics.

In Siachen and Ladakh, helicopters and transports resupply forward posts, and advanced landing grounds such as Daulat Beg Oldi and Nyoma extend reach. IAF aircraft also support civil authorities for VIP and election duties and take part in the annual Air Force Day flypast.

Analysis, practice and revision

Perspectives, controversies and facts

Interpretation Squadron numbers. One view says the IAF must reach 42 squadrons to be credible against two adversaries. A second view says missiles, drones, AEW&C and air defence can substitute for some fighters, so the target should be capability, not a number set decades ago.

Interpretation Import or indigenise. Supporters of buying foreign fighters stress speed. Supporters of the Tejas and AMCA stress self-reliance and the cost of foreign dependence. The reality is a mix, with a long period of overlap.

Interpretation Manned versus unmanned. Drones and loyal wingmen are cheaper and expendable, but need robust communications and cannot yet replace the judgement of a pilot in a contested airspace.

Interpretation Theatre commands and air power. The IAF has voiced concern about splitting scarce assets, while supporters of reform point to the benefits of integrated planning.

Disputed Combat claims. Claims on both sides about aircraft downed in 2019 and 2025 differ. A careful answer states what each side claims and notes that independent confirmation is limited.

Lesser-known facts

  • Established The IAF's first operational flight in 1933 had just six Indian officers and 19 airmen.
  • Established Marshal of the IAF Arjan Singh led the service in the 1965 war while still in his mid-40s.
  • Established Only one Param Vir Chakra has gone to an IAF airman: Flying Officer Nirmal Jit Singh Sekhon (1971).
  • Established The Central Air Command is headquartered at Prayagraj, linking this handbook to the city profile (handbook 27).

Scenarios and strategic options

Presented neutrally, without recommending a political position:

Managed deterrence

Border arrangements with China hold, the LoC ceasefire mostly holds, and deliveries of Tejas Mk1A and new missiles gradually narrow the gap.

Limited air-missile exchange

A terror attack triggers a short, intense exchange of drones, missiles and strikes, with outside pressure to de-escalate. Air defence and stand-off weapons matter most.

Two-front stretch

A crisis on the LAC coincides with tension on the western front. Squadron numbers, basing in depth and tankers are tested.

Technology-led transformation

Loyal wingmen, AI-assisted command, space-based sensing and drone swarms reshape the force, but only if procurement, engines and training keep pace.

Answer framework and worked example

For any "Is X the right measure or policy?" question, move through definition, location, evidence, contest, analysis, test, position, concession and close. One compressed example follows.

Worked example: "Is 42 squadrons the right measure of IAF strength?"

Define
The sanctioned number of fighter squadrons, a planning benchmark set to cover a two-front war.
Locate
Serving strength has been about 29-31, falling as older aircraft retire. Re-verify
Establish
Fewer aircraft mean less flexibility, especially across two fronts.
Contest
Critics say the number ignores AEW&C, tankers, missiles and electronic warfare.
Analyse
Force multipliers raise each squadron's effect, but cannot be in two places at once.
Test
Compare serviceability, stand-off weapon stocks and basing depth, not squadrons alone.
Position
The number is a useful warning light but not a complete measure.
Concede
Without more fighters, even strong multipliers leave a coverage gap.
Close
Use both measures: squadrons for mass, multipliers for effect.
