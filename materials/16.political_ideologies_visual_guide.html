02 · The economic family

Start with productive assets: factories, land, infrastructure, companies, capital. Then ask who owns or controls them and how resources are allocated.

Economic system
Capitalism
PRIVATE OWNERSHIP + MARKETS + PROFIT MOTIVE

Individuals and firms can own productive assets and make many economic decisions through markets. Private enterprise and competition are central features.

Think: “I can own the company, take the risk and keep the profit.”
Economic / political tradition
Socialism
SOCIAL / COLLECTIVE CONTROL OF PRODUCTION

A broad family of ideas favouring greater social, collective, public or worker control over economic resources. Different socialist traditions disagree about the exact ownership model.

Think: “Economic power should be shared and serve society more broadly.”
Theoretical end-state
Communism
COMMON OWNERSHIP + CLASSLESS SOCIETY

In Marxist theory, full communism is a classless and ultimately stateless society based on common ownership. Historical “communist states” did not simply equal this theoretical end-state.

Think: “The end goal is a classless society with common ownership.”
Policy model
Social Democracy
MARKETS + WELFARE + REGULATION + REDISTRIBUTION

A market economy remains, but the state uses taxation, welfare, regulation and public services to reduce insecurity and inequality.

Think: “Keep markets, but build a strong social safety net.”

03 · Who has political power?

These labels answer a different question from capitalism/socialism: who can make decisions, how leaders are chosen, and how much competition or freedom exists?

Political system

Democracy

Political authority is grounded in the people, with participation and representative institutions at its core.

PEOPLE → ELECTIONS / PARTICIPATION → GOVERNMENT
Political system

Authoritarianism

Political power is concentrated and meaningful political competition or accountability is substantially restricted.

CONCENTRATED POWER + LIMITED COMPETITION
Political system

Dictatorship

Power is concentrated in one ruler or a small group, with weak mechanisms for removing or constraining them through genuine political competition.

ONE RULER / SMALL GROUP → HIGH POLITICAL CONTROL
Extreme form

Totalitarianism

Goes beyond political control toward extensive attempts to control society, information, organisations and public life.

POLITICAL CONTROL → BROAD SOCIAL CONTROL
Don't confuse

Democracy ≠ Capitalism

You can have democratic politics with substantial welfare and public ownership. You can also have capitalist markets under authoritarian governments.

Don't confuse

Dictatorship ≠ Communism

A dictatorship describes concentration of political power. Communism describes an economic/social theory and historical political tradition. A system can combine authoritarian rule with different economic models.

04 · What values shape politics?

These terms are best understood as ideological traditions. Their exact meaning can change across countries and periods.

Individual liberty

Liberalism

Strong emphasis on individual rights, liberty, freedom of thought/expression and limits on arbitrary government power.

Core instinct: “Protect the individual from arbitrary power.”
Tradition & change

Conservatism

Values continuity, established institutions, social stability and caution about rapid or sweeping change.

Core instinct: “Change carefully; institutions may contain hard-won wisdom.”
Nation

Nationalism

Places the nation and national identity at the centre of political loyalty, self-determination or state-building.

Core instinct: “The nation should be an important political community.”
People vs elite

Populism

Frames politics as a conflict between “ordinary people” and “the elite.” It can appear on both the left and the right.

Core instinct: “Politics should reflect the real people, not an insulated elite.”
Radical nationalism

Fascism

A specific historical ideological family associated with authoritarian leadership, extreme nationalism, militarism, hierarchy and rejection of liberal-democratic pluralism.

Core instinct: “Nation + authority + mobilisation above liberal pluralism.”
Maximum liberty

Libertarianism

Places especially strong weight on individual freedom and limiting coercive state power, with different schools differing on economics and social questions.

Core instinct: “Why should the state be able to decide this?”
State & religion

Secularism

A political/legal order that does not derive its authority from religious rule; the relationship between religion and state differs by country.

Core instinct: “Government should not be governed by religious authority as such.”
Religion in rule

Theocracy

A system in which religious authority or divine law is closely tied to political authority and the state's legal order.

Core instinct: “Religious authority is foundational to political rule.”

05 · How is the state structured?

These terms describe institutional architecture rather than left/right economics.

Head of state

Republic

A non-monarchical state structure in which the head of state is not a hereditary monarch. A republic can also be democratic.

Head of state

Monarchy

A state with a monarch as head of state, often hereditary. Monarchies can be absolute or constitutional.

Territorial power

Federalism

Constitutionally recognised powers are divided between a national government and regional/state governments.

Territorial power

Unitary system

Constitutional authority is more strongly concentrated at the national level, though local governments may still exercise substantial delegated powers.

Imperialism

Extension of a state's power or influence beyond its own borders through political, economic or military means.

POWER OF STATE A → INFLUENCE / CONTROL BEYOND ITS BORDERS

Colonialism

A historical form of external domination involving political control of territory and populations, often with settlement and economic exploitation.

EXTERNAL RULE + TERRITORIAL CONTROL + COLONIAL ADMINISTRATION
Republic vs democracy“Republic” and “democracy” are not opposites. Republic describes a state form; democracy describes how political authority is grounded and exercised.

06 · Decode India’s constitutional description

The Preamble calls India a Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic. Each adjective answers a different question.

Sovereign
India is an independent state that exercises supreme authority over its territory and conducts its own external relations.
Socialist
A constitutional commitment to social and economic justice; in practice, India has a mixed/market economy rather than a Soviet-style command economy.
Secular
The constitutional order does not establish a theocratic state; religion and state are related differently than in a system governed by religious authority.
Democratic
Political authority rests on democratic institutions, participation and representative government.
Republic
India does not have a hereditary monarch as its head of state; the President is the constitutional head of the Union.
The takeaway
One sentence can contain multiple dimensions. Never assume “socialist,” “democratic,” and “republic” are competing labels.
