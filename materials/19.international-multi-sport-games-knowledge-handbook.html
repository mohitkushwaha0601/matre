Using this handbook

This guide is deliberately short. For each major multi-sport event it gives three things: origin (who started it and why), history (the milestones worth remembering) and eminence (why it matters, and India's place in it).

Learn the anchor years and founders first. Then, for each Games, be ready to say one sentence on India. Interviewers rarely want medal tables; they want to see that you can link sport to history, diplomacy and national character.

Labels used

Established settled record · Interpretation analytical judgement · Disputed contested claim · Re-verify check before quoting (mainly recent or future events).

Part I · Foundations

Where multi-sport Games began

Ancient roots & the revival

The ancient Olympic Games were held at Olympia, Greece, in honour of Zeus, traditionally from 776 BC. They took place every four years (an "Olympiad"), and a sacred truce let athletes travel safely. Events grew from a single sprint (the stadion) to wrestling, boxing, chariot racing and the pentathlon. The Games ended around AD 393, when Roman Emperor Theodosius I banned pagan festivals.

The modern revival came from French educator Baron Pierre de Coubertin, who believed sport could build character and international understanding. At a congress at the Sorbonne, Paris, on 23 June 1894, the International Olympic Committee (IOC) was founded. 23 June is now Olympic Day. The first modern Games followed in Athens, 1896.

Coubertin's lasting ideas

  • "The important thing in life is not the triumph but the struggle." (a creed he popularised)
  • The five interlocking rings (designed 1913, first flown at Antwerp 1920) stand for the union of the five continents. Coubertin chose the six colours, including the white background, because together they could reproduce every national flag of his time.
  • Amateurism: an ideal that shaped the Games until professionals were gradually admitted from the 1980s.

The family of Games

Multi-sport events fall into a few clear families. Most follow the Olympic model (opening ceremony, athletes' village, flame or baton, medal table), but they differ in membership and purpose.

Interpretation Almost every family copied the Olympic template within a few decades of 1896. This shows how fast Coubertin's model became the world's standard format for international sport.

Part II · Global Games

The Olympic family and other world events

Summer Olympics

The world's largest sporting event: more than 200 National Olympic Committees, about 10,500 athletes and over 300 events. The IOC is based in Lausanne, Switzerland.

  • 1896Athens: 14 nations, about 241 athletes, men only.
  • 1900Paris: women compete for the first time.
  • 1916, 40, 44Cancelled because of the World Wars.
  • 1920Antwerp: Olympic flag and athletes' oath introduced.
  • 1928Amsterdam: Olympic flame lit; India's first hockey gold.
  • 1936Berlin: first torch relay from Olympia.
  • 2021Tokyo 2020 held a year late due to COVID-19; "Communiter" added to the motto.
  • 2024Paris: first Games with equal numbers of male and female athletes.
  • NextLos Angeles 2028 (cricket returns), Brisbane 2032.

India at the Olympics

  • First appearance: Norman Pritchard, 1900 (two silvers); first official team, 1920.
  • Eight hockey golds (1928-1956, 1964, 1980).
  • First individual medal after independence: K.D. Jadhav, wrestling bronze, 1952.
  • Individual gold: Abhinav Bindra (shooting, 2008) and Neeraj Chopra (javelin, Tokyo 2020).
  • Best haul: 7 medals at Tokyo 2020; 6 at Paris 2024.

Winter Olympics

Winter sports first appeared within the Summer Games (figure skating in 1908, ice hockey in 1920). A separate "International Winter Sports Week" at Chamonix, France, 1924 was later recognised as the first Winter Olympics.

Until 1992, Summer and Winter Games were held in the same year. From Lillehammer 1994 they were staggered two years apart, so there is an Olympics every two years. Winter Games are dominated by Norway, Germany, the USA and Canada.

Recent and next hosts

  • 2022 Beijing (first city to host both Summer and Winter Games)
  • 2026 Milano Cortina, Italy (February 2026)
  • 2030 French Alps; 2034 Salt Lake City

India

India first competed in 1964 (Innsbruck). Luger Shiva Keshavan competed at six Games. India has never won a Winter medal; its presence is small but symbolic of Himalayan sport.

Paralympics

The Paralympic movement began with German-British neurologist Sir Ludwig Guttmann at Stoke Mandeville Hospital, England. On 29 July 1948, the opening day of the London Olympics, he held an archery contest for wheelchair-using war veterans: the Stoke Mandeville Games.

The first Paralympics, open to athletes other than veterans, took place in Rome, 1960, just after the Olympics. Since Seoul 1988 the Paralympics have used the same host city and venues as the Olympics. The International Paralympic Committee (IPC), founded in 1989, is based in Bonn. Its symbol is the three agitos ("I move").

India's Paralympic rise

First gold: Murlikant Petkar, swimming, 1972. A former Army jawan wounded in the 1965 war, his story is excellent SSB material. India won 19 medals at Tokyo 2020 and a record 29 medals (7 gold) at Paris 2024, far more than its Olympic tally.

Youth Olympic Games

Proposed by IOC President Jacques Rogge to bring the Olympic spirit to athletes aged 15-18, the Youth Olympic Games (YOG) began in Singapore, 2010, with a Winter edition in Innsbruck in 2012. They combine competition with education and culture programmes, and test new formats such as mixed-nation teams.

The 2026 Summer YOG were scheduled for Dakar, Senegal, the first Olympic event ever held in Africa Re-verify dates.

India: India won 13 medals (3 gold) at Buenos Aires 2018, its best YOG result; shooter Manu Bhaker was among the gold medallists. Jeremy Lalrinnunga (weightlifting) became India's first YOG gold medallist. Many YOG athletes later become senior stars.

University, military & other world Games

Several smaller world events serve specific communities. Know their founder, first edition and purpose.

World University Games
FISU · student athletes
First as "Universiade": Turin 1959

Run by the International University Sports Federation. Indian university athletes compete regularly.

Military World Games
CISM · armed forces athletes
First: Rome 1995

Organised by the International Military Sports Council (founded 1948) under the motto "Friendship through Sport". Indian Services teams take part.

Deaflympics
ICSD · deaf athletes
First: Paris 1924

The oldest multi-sport event for athletes with a disability; the 2025 edition in Tokyo marked its centenary.

Special Olympics
Athletes with intellectual disabilities
First: Chicago 1968

Founded by Eunice Kennedy Shriver; values participation over winning. India competes through Special Olympics Bharat.

The World Games
IWGA · non-Olympic sports
First: Santa Clara 1981

For sports outside the Olympic programme (e.g. korfball, ju-jitsu, tug of war). Chengdu hosted in 2025.

Goodwill Games
Cold War bridge (defunct)
Moscow 1986 to Brisbane 2001

Created by Ted Turner after the 1980 and 1984 Olympic boycotts to bring US and Soviet athletes together.

Part III · Commonwealth, continental & regional

Games built on history and geography

Commonwealth Games

The idea of an "Empire" sports festival was floated in the 1890s and tested at the 1911 Festival of Empire in London. Canadian journalist Bobby Robinson turned it into reality: the first British Empire Games were held in Hamilton, Canada, in 1930, with 11 nations.

Changing names, changing Commonwealth
YearsName
1930-1950British Empire Games
1954-1966British Empire and Commonwealth Games
1970-1974British Commonwealth Games
1978-Commonwealth Games

Known as the "Friendly Games", they are run by the Commonwealth Games Federation (now branded Commonwealth Sport) in London. Unique features: the King's (formerly Queen's) Baton Relay since 1958, and full integration of para-sport events into the main programme since Manchester 2002. Glasgow hosted a scaled-down 2026 edition with about 10 sports Re-verify, which left out several of India's strongest sports, including hockey, wrestling, shooting and badminton.

India and the Commonwealth Games

  • First appearance: London 1934. First gold: Milkha Singh, 440 yards, Cardiff 1958.
  • Hosted Delhi 2010: best-ever 101 medals (38 gold), 2nd place, but overshadowed by delays and corruption allegations.
  • Birmingham 2022: 61 medals (22 gold), 4th.
  • Ahmedabad was chosen to host the 2030 centenary Games Re-verify.

Asian Games

Asia's largest multi-sport event, and the one with the strongest Indian roots. Indian sports administrator Guru Dutt Sondhi proposed it at the 1948 London Olympics; the Asian Games Federation was founded in New Delhi in 1949; and the first Games were held in New Delhi in March 1951. The motto, "Ever Onward", came from Jawaharlal Nehru.

Since 1982 the Games have been run by the Olympic Council of Asia (Kuwait City, 45 members). They often have more events than the Olympics, including Asian sports such as kabaddi, wushu and sepaktakraw. Japan led the medal table until 1978; China has led every edition since 1982.

India: hosted 1951 and 1982 (when colour TV came to India); 2nd in 1951; best tally 107 medals (28 gold), 4th place at Hangzhou 2022. The 20th edition was held in Aichi-Nagoya, Japan, in 2026. See the Asian Games handbook for detail.

Other continental Games

Every continent now has its own Games, usually held the year before or after the Olympics and often used as Olympic qualifiers.

Pattern to notice

Interpretation The 1950s-60s wave (Asian 1951, Pan American 1951, African 1965) coincided with decolonisation: new nations used sport to assert identity. Europe, already well served by the Olympics, created its own Games only in 2015.

South Asian & cultural Games

The South Asian Games began as the South Asian Federation (SAF) Games in Kathmandu, 1984, and were renamed in 2004. Members are the eight SAARC nations. India hosted in 1987 (Calcutta), 1995 (Madras) and 2016 (Guwahati-Shillong) and almost always tops the table. The next edition, planned for Pakistan, has faced repeated delays Re-verify.

SEA Games

Southeast Asia, since 1959 (Bangkok, as the SEAP Games).

Jeux de la Francophonie

French-speaking nations, since 1989 (Morocco); sport and culture together.

Islamic Solidarity Games

Members of the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation, since 2005 (Mecca).

Interpretation For India, the South Asian Games are less about medals and more about neighbourhood diplomacy, as a forum where sporting ties continue even when political relations in SAARC are frozen.

Part IV · Eminence

Why the Games matter

Why the Games matter

Multi-sport Games matter far beyond sport. They are stages for national pride, diplomacy, protest and development.

Soft power & nation-branding

Tokyo 1964 announced Japan's post-war recovery; Seoul 1988 South Korea's democratic and economic rise; Beijing 2008 China's arrival as a great power.

Politics on the podium

Berlin 1936 (Nazi propaganda, Jesse Owens's four golds); Mexico City 1968 (Black Power salute); Munich 1972 (11 Israeli team members killed by terrorists).

Boycotts

Montreal 1976 (African nations, over apartheid-related sporting contacts); Moscow 1980 (US-led, over Afghanistan); Los Angeles 1984 (Soviet bloc); Edinburgh 1986 Commonwealth Games (32 nations, over policy toward South Africa).

Development & legacy

Barcelona 1992 is the classic success story of urban renewal. Montreal took about 30 years to pay off its 1976 debt, and Athens 2004 left many unused venues.

Interview edge

If asked "Why do countries spend so much to host the Games?", give both sides: prestige, infrastructure and investment on one hand; debt, displacement and empty venues on the other. Then add the Indian angle: Delhi 1982 (positive legacy), Delhi 2010 (cautionary tale), and the 2030 and 2036 ambitions ahead.

Analysis, practice and revision

Perspectives, controversies & lesser-known facts

Supporters' view

Interpretation The Games promote peace, inclusion and health, inspire young people, and give smaller nations a world stage. The Paralympic and Special Olympic movements have changed how societies see disability.

Critics' view

Interpretation Hosting costs often overrun, venues sit unused, local residents can be displaced, and the Games are used for "sportswashing" by governments seeking to polish their image.

Recurring controversies

  • Doping: from Ben Johnson (Seoul 1988) to Russia's state-run programme exposed after Sochi 2014. Established
  • Neutral athletes: Russian and Belarusian athletes competed as "Individual Neutral Athletes" at Paris 2024 after the invasion of Ukraine. Disputed whether this is fair or too lenient.
  • Relevance of the Commonwealth Games: rising costs, withdrawn hosts (Durban 2022, Victoria 2026) and a shrinking sports list. Disputed
  • Gender eligibility rules in athletics and boxing remain contested.

Lesser-known facts

  • Art competitions (painting, music, literature) were part of the Olympics from 1912 to 1948.
  • Indonesia held the rival GANEFO (Games of the New Emerging Forces) in 1963 after being suspended by the IOC.
  • South Africa was excluded from the Olympics from 1964 to 1988 because of apartheid, returning in 1992.
  • India's Norman Pritchard later became a Hollywood silent-film actor.

Scenarios & India's options

India now wants to move from participant to host. The 2030 Commonwealth Games in Ahmedabad and an Indian Olympic Association letter of intent for the 2036 Olympics Re-verify status are the two big markers.

Successful host

A well-run 2030 CWG strengthens India's 2036 Olympic bid and leaves lasting infrastructure.

Mixed outcome

The Games run smoothly but at high cost; debate shifts to whether an Olympic bid is affordable.

Delhi 2010 repeated

Delays or scandal damage credibility and set back Olympic ambitions by a decade or more.

Worked example: Q10, "Should India bid for the 2036 Olympics?"

Define
An Olympic bid is a decade-long national project, not a one-off event.
For
Prestige, infrastructure, a boost to sport participation, and a showcase for a $4-trillion-plus economy.
Against
Cost overruns (Montreal, Athens), the Delhi 2010 experience, and competing needs in health and education.
Position
Yes, but conditionally: only with transparent budgets, reuse of existing venues, and a proven record from CWG 2030.
Close
"Hosting should crown a sporting culture, not substitute for building one."

Glossary

IOC
International Olympic Committee; founded 1894, based in Lausanne.
IPC
International Paralympic Committee; founded 1989, based in Bonn.
NOC
National Olympic Committee; India's is the Indian Olympic Association (IOA).
Olympiad
The four-year period between Summer Olympic Games.
Olympic Truce
Ancient tradition, revived by UN resolutions, of halting conflict during the Games.
Agitos
The three-arc Paralympic symbol, Latin for "I move".
Baton Relay
The Commonwealth Games equivalent of the Olympic torch relay, carrying the monarch's message.
CGF / Commonwealth Sport
The body that runs the Commonwealth Games, based in London.
OCA
Olympic Council of Asia; runs the Asian Games, based in Kuwait City.
FISU
International University Sports Federation; runs the World University Games.
CISM
International Military Sports Council; runs the Military World Games.
Sportswashing
Using sporting events to improve a government's reputation.
