Using this handbook

This is a compact reference on the Asian Games in general, the 20th edition held in Aichi Prefecture and Nagoya, Japan (19 September - 4 October 2026), and India's long relationship with Asia's largest multi-sport event. Part I covers origins briefly; Parts II and III carry the weight; the toolkit turns it into interview material.

Sport questions at an SSB interview are rarely about trivia. The interviewing officer wants to see whether you follow national events, whether you can connect a medal table to policy (funding, talent identification, the armed forces' role), and whether you can talk about sportsmanship and pressure with some personal conviction. Learn the anchor numbers, then practise saying why they matter.

A note on 2026 figures

This handbook was compiled as the 2026 Games closed. Historic numbers are well established; anything about the 2026 programme, venues, India's contingent and final results is marked Re-verify and should be checked against the Olympic Council of Asia (OCA), the Indian Olympic Association (IOA) and Sports Authority of India (SAI) releases before you quote it. Saying "I would want to verify the exact count, but roughly…" is a perfectly good interview answer.

Part I · Origins

Where the Asian Games came from

Origins of the Asian Games

The Asian Games grew out of two regional experiments and one Indian idea: that newly independent Asia should have its own festival of sport, separate from European-dominated competition.

  • 1913-1934Far Eastern Championship Games (first in Manila) among the Philippines, China and Japan; collapsed in 1934 over Japan's insistence on admitting its puppet state Manchukuo.
  • 1934Western Asiatic Games in New Delhi — India, Afghanistan, Ceylon and Palestine — driven by Indian sports administrator Guru Dutt Sondhi. A second edition planned for Tel Aviv never took place.
  • 1948At the London Olympics, Sondhi proposed an Asian Games to Asian team leaders; the idea won support from the Philippines, Burma, Ceylon, China, Korea and others.
  • 13 Feb 1949The Asian Games Federation was formally founded in New Delhi.
  • 4-11 Mar 1951First Asian Games, New Delhi (postponed from 1950) at the National Stadium, now the Major Dhyan Chand National Stadium. Opened by President Rajendra Prasad; 11 nations, about 489 athletes, 6 sports.

Japan topped the first table with 24 golds; India finished second with 51 medals (15 gold), still its best-ever rank. The motto "Ever Onward", suggested by Jawaharlal Nehru, captured the mood of a decolonising continent.

Interview edge

If asked "What is India's link to the Asian Games?", don't stop at "India hosted it twice." The stronger answer: an Indian proposed it, the governing federation was founded in Delhi, India hosted the first edition, and Nehru gave it its motto. India was a founder, not just a participant.

The Olympic Council of Asia

The Asian Games Federation ran the Games until 1981-82, when it was replaced by the Olympic Council of Asia (OCA), founded at a meeting in New Delhi in November 1981 and headquartered in Kuwait City. The OCA is recognised by the International Olympic Committee as Asia's continental association and has 45 member National Olympic Committees.

The Indian Olympic Association (IOA) is India's NOC and sends the contingent; the Union Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports, through SAI, funds most preparation and clears the final list. That split — IOA selects and enters, the government funds and approves — explains many of India's selection debates.

The Asian Para Games are run separately by the Asian Paralympic Committee, usually in the same host city a few weeks after the main Games.

Memory hooks

  • Bangkok hosted four times (1966, 1970, 1978, 1998) — twice as a rescue host.
  • Japan's 2026 edition is its third (1958, 1994, 2026); India has hosted twice (1951, 1982).
  • Doha 2030 and Riyadh 2034 were awarded together in December 2020 — a sign of the Gulf's sports-hosting push.

Programme, politics & evolution

The Asian Games have always carried a wider and more regional programme than the Olympics. Alongside Olympic sports, they include disciplines with deep Asian roots — kabaddi, sepaktakraw, wushu, kurash, jujitsu, soft tennis, dragon boat — and, more recently, cricket and esports. The 1951 Games had 6 sports; Hangzhou had 40 sports and 481 events, more than the Olympics.

Programme milestones

  • 1990 — kabaddi debuts; India wins every men's gold until Iran breaks the run in 2018.
  • 2010 — cricket debuts; India sends no teams in 2010 or 2014.
  • 2018 — esports as a demonstration event.
  • 2022 — esports awarded medals; India wins both cricket golds on debut.

Politics at the Games

  • 1962 — Indonesia refused visas to Israel and Taiwan; the IOC suspended Indonesia.
  • 1974 — PRC admitted; Taiwan left the Asian Games movement, returning in 1990 as "Chinese Taipei".
  • 1976-81 — Israel expelled; it now competes in European events.
  • 2002-18 — joint Korean marches and a unified women's basketball team in 2018.
Analytical point

Interpretation The Asian Games are a mirror of Asian geopolitics: decolonisation in 1951, Cold War recognition battles in the 1960s-70s, China's rise from 1982, and today's Gulf hosting push. A good interview answer links one sporting fact to one strategic trend.

Part II · Aichi-Nagoya 2026

The 20th Asian Games in Japan

The host and the bid

The 20th Asian Games were held from 19 September to 4 October 2026, jointly hosted by Aichi Prefecture and its capital, Nagoya, Japan's fourth-largest city and the heart of its automotive and manufacturing belt (Toyota's home region).

  • Sep 2016OCA General Assembly in Da Nang, Vietnam, awards the 2026 Games to Aichi-Nagoya, the only bidder.
  • 2020-23COVID-19 pushes Hangzhou 2022 to 2023, compressing the gap before 2026 to three years.
  • 2023-25Organisers revise the cost plan, including reported reliance on existing venues and alternatives to a purpose-built athletes' village Re-verify.
  • 19 Sep 2026Opening ceremony; the main stadium is the redeveloped Mizuho athletics stadium in Nagoya Re-verify.
  • 4 Oct 2026Closing ceremony; the OCA flag passes to Doha for 2030.

This was Japan's third Asian Games, after Tokyo 1958 and Hiroshima 1994, and its first since hosting the Tokyo 2020 Olympics (held 2021). That sequencing matters: Japan arrived with venue experience, high public sensitivity to mega-event costs after Tokyo 2020's overruns, and a reputation for organisational discipline.

Why Aichi?

Interpretation Aichi had lost the 1988 Olympic bid to Seoul and hosted Expo 2005. The Asian Games offered a regional showcase, infrastructure renewal and a chance to brand central Japan internationally — the same motivations that drove Delhi in 1982 and Guangzhou in 2010.

Sports, venues & the hosting model

The OCA approved a programme of about 41 sports and roughly 460 events Re-verify, mixing Olympic disciplines with Asian-specific ones. For India, the most relevant inclusions were cricket (T20 format, men and women), kabaddi, hockey, esports, wushu, squash and sepaktakraw.

Venue model

Heavy use of existing arenas across Aichi, with some events in neighbouring prefectures; a lean approach shaped by post-Tokyo 2020 cost scrutiny Re-verify.

Athlete housing

Organisers reportedly opted against a single new athletes' village, considering hotels and moored cruise ships instead Re-verify.

Olympic linkage

Some events served as Asian qualifiers or ranking events for the Los Angeles 2028 Olympics, where cricket and squash also feature Re-verify.

Storylines & the Asian Para Games

Three big themes framed Aichi-Nagoya 2026, regardless of individual results:

  1. China-Japan-Korea at the top. China's lead in the medal table has been unbroken since 1982; Japan as host aimed to narrow the gap, as hosts usually do. India's realistic target is the next tier: consolidating fourth place or staying close to it.
  2. The cost question. After Tokyo 2020's overruns and public opposition, Japan tried to show a mega-event could be run lean. That debate now shapes Doha 2030, Riyadh 2034 and India's own 2036 Olympic ambitions.
  3. Asia's sporting map widening. Gulf states are emerging as hosts, Central Asian nations (Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan) are rising powers in combat sports, and the OCA has explored closer ties with Oceania Re-verify.

Asian Para Games 2026

The 5th Asian Para Games were scheduled in Aichi-Nagoya in October 2026, after the main Games Re-verify dates. India's para-sport has risen fast: 111 medals at Hangzhou 2022 Asian Para Games (29 gold), then 29 medals at the Paris 2024 Paralympics. Mention para-athletes whenever you discuss India's sporting progress — it is the most striking growth story of the decade.

Part III · India and the Games

Participation, performance and spirit

India's journey, 1951-2018

India has competed at every Asian Games. Its story has three phases: early strength as a founder, a long plateau from the late 1980s, and a sharp rise since 2010 driven by funding, science and a wider talent base.

Interview edge

Asked "Is India a sporting power?", avoid both triumphalism and cynicism. A balanced line: "India has moved from eighth to fourth in Asia in one cycle and crossed 100 medals, but China won roughly seven times as many golds at Hangzhou. We are a rising second-tier power with depth in a few sports, not yet broad-based."

Hangzhou: "Is baar sau paar"

India travelled to Hangzhou (23 September - 8 October 2023) with the slogan "Is baar sau paar" — "this time, past a hundred". It finished with 107 medals (28 gold, 38 silver, 41 bronze) and fourth place, its best tally ever and its best rank since 1962. The medal table was led by China (201 gold, 383 total), Japan and South Korea.

Interview edge

Before your interview, write down India's final 2026 tally, rank, two individual stories and one disappointment. Officers often ask, "Which Asian Games performance inspired you most?" A specific name and a specific moment beat a general answer every time.

The spirit: sport & character

The "spirit" of the Games is best shown through moments, not slogans. These are useful because they link sport to officer-like qualities.

Composure under pressure

At Hangzhou, Neeraj Chopra's first javelin throw went unmeasured because of a technical glitch. He waited, threw again and won gold. Hurdler Jyothi Yarraji protested calmly after a disputed false-start ruling and was later awarded silver.

Respect for the rival

Neeraj Chopra and Pakistan's Arshad Nadeem shared the podium at Jakarta 2018 and have spoken of each other warmly ever since. Sport can model respect even when political relations are cold.

Rising from modest roots

Many medallists come from farming families and small towns: Parul Chaudhary (Meerut), Annu Rani (a village near Meerut), Avinash Sable (Beed, Maharashtra). Their stories show what access to coaching and funding can unlock.

The uniform behind the medal

The armed forces have long been India's quiet sports academy — from Milkha Singh to Neeraj Chopra and Avinash Sable. The Army's Mission Olympic Wing gives athletes a career, discipline and long-term support.

SSB link

Map sport to OLQs without forcing it: determination (years of training for one event), courage (competing hurt), cooperation (relays, team sports), self-confidence (handling a bad call), sense of responsibility (representing the country). Then add your own example from a game you played.

Sport, soft power & hosting

The Asian Games are also a stage for diplomacy. Three themes are relevant to India.

1. Sport and bilateral friction

Established Before Hangzhou 2023, three women wushu athletes from Arunachal Pradesh did not receive accreditation to enter China. India lodged a strong protest and the then sports minister cancelled his visit. China's position on Arunachal Pradesh was the underlying issue. India-Pakistan finals (hockey, squash, cricket) draw huge audiences and political attention.

2. Hosting as nation-branding

Delhi 1982 brought flyovers, stadiums, the Asian Games Village and colour television to India, and led to the creation of SAI. The 2010 Commonwealth Games in Delhi showed the opposite risk: delays and corruption allegations. India now aims at bigger events: Ahmedabad was chosen to host the 2030 Commonwealth Games, and the IOA has submitted a letter of intent for the 2036 Olympics Re-verify status. India has not hosted the Asian Games since 1982.

3. Soft power

Interpretation Medals feed national pride, attract investment into sport and support India's image as a confident, youthful nation. Critics argue that hosting money is better spent on grassroots facilities. Both views are legitimate; the strongest answer weighs legacy use against cost.

Knowledge toolkit

Analysis, practice and revision

Perspectives, controversies & lesser-known facts

The optimistic reading

Interpretation India's rise from 70 to 107 medals in one cycle shows that sustained funding, sports science and athlete-centred schemes work. A broader middle class, Khelo India and the services' programmes have widened the talent base. The trend line points upward.

The sceptical reading

Interpretation Asian Games medals overstate Olympic strength because several Indian golds come in non-Olympic events. China's lead is enormous, governance disputes recur, and per-capita performance remains modest for a country of 1.4 billion people.

Recurring controversies

  • Selection: disputes over trials, exemptions and the government's "top-eight in Asia" rule for team sports. Disputed
  • Judging: officiating rows in boxing, athletics and combat sports recur at most editions.
  • Cost of hosting: whether mega-events justify public spending, debated in Japan, Indonesia and India alike. Disputed
  • Politics: visa and accreditation denials, from Jakarta 1962 to Hangzhou 2023.

Lesser-known facts

  • The 1951 Games were delayed from 1950 because preparations were not ready.
  • India's best-ever rank (2nd) came at the first Games.
  • India's only gold at Beijing 1990 came in kabaddi, the sport's debut year.
  • SAI exists largely because Delhi needed to manage its 1982 Asian Games venues.
  • The Asian Games often have more events than the Summer Olympics.

Scenarios & India's options

Three plausible pathways for India's Asian Games performance toward Doha 2030, presented without preference:

Consolidation

India holds roughly fourth place and 100+ medals, adding depth in athletics, shooting and archery but making little headway in swimming or gymnastics.

Breakthrough

Khelo India graduates mature, new governance laws reduce federation disputes, and India challenges South Korea or Japan for a top-three finish.

Plateau

Governance rows, doping cases or funding fatigue after LA 2028 slow progress; India's tally slips back toward the 70-90 range.

Worked example — Q7: Asian Games vs Olympic medals

Define
Asian Games medals measure continental standing; Olympic medals measure global standing.
Establish
India won 107 Asian Games medals at Hangzhou but six at the Paris 2024 Olympics.
Analyse
Some Asian Games golds come in non-Olympic events, and fields are weaker in some sports. But the Asian Games build depth, competitive experience and qualification pathways.
Concede
Medal tables shape funding and public attention, so Asian Games success should not be dismissed.
Close
"Both matter: the Asian Games are the proving ground, the Olympics the final test. The goal should be turning Asian podiums into Olympic ones."

Glossary

OCA
Olympic Council of Asia; Asia's IOC-recognised continental body, based in Kuwait City.
NOC
National Olympic Committee; India's is the Indian Olympic Association (IOA).
NSF
National Sports Federation; governs a single sport in India (e.g. AFI for athletics).
SAI
Sports Authority of India; the government body running training centres and schemes.
TOPS
Target Olympic Podium Scheme; targeted funding for elite athletes, launched 2014.
Khelo India
Grassroots and youth sports programme with national games and scholarships.
Mission Olympic Wing
The Indian Army's elite-sport programme, centred on the Army Sports Institute, Pune.
Chef de mission
The official leading a national contingent at a multi-sport Games.
Medal table
Ranking of nations by gold medals first, then silver, then bronze.
Asian Para Games
Multi-sport event for athletes with impairments, organised by the Asian Paralympic Committee.
NADA / WADA
National and World Anti-Doping Agencies, responsible for testing and sanctions.
"Ever Onward"
The Asian Games motto, suggested by Jawaharlal Nehru in 1951.
